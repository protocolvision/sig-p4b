Protocol Play

Games and simulations for practicing protocol vision. Reading about swarms and watching protocols only goes so far. Play lets you set the rules yourself and watch what a group of people and agents does with them. Some games run as session features, and others are workshop formats that can travel.

Related formats from past workshops: AI Futurama (red and blue teams, 30-year roadmaps) and AI Kitcraft (hands-on tooling).

Swarm Simulation in design

A short game to practice what the syllabus argues. You run Kestrel Widget Works, a made-up AI-native company whose work is done by a swarm: a few centaur employees and many agents that coordinate through what they emit. Your job is not to do the work. It is to set the guardrails that let the swarm produce widgets without going rogue.

The company

  • The swarmAgents that take orders, buy parts, schedule production, check quality, answer customers, and file reports. A handful of human centaurs work alongside them.
  • The environmentWhat the swarm coordinates through: an order board, an inventory log, a quality notes channel, supplier feeds, and a public disclosure record. There are no meetings and no org chart.
  • The goalShip widgets on time, at a profit, for twelve simulated weeks.

Your guardrails

Before each round you choose and tune a limited set of guardrails. Every guardrail has a cost: it slows the swarm, costs money, or takes a person's attention.

  • Scopes and budgets: what each agent may touch, spend, and sign.
  • Emission rules: what must be posted to the shared board, who can read it, and how long it stays.
  • Thresholds and quorums: how many agents or people must agree before an order, a payment, or a public statement goes out.
  • Human sign-off: which decisions wait for a centaur, and how many such decisions each person can handle before they stop really checking.
  • Evals: which outputs are scored against real outcomes, and how often.
  • Circuit breakers: what pauses the swarm, and who can restart it.

What goes wrong

Each round draws events based on the syllabus readings and on the 2026 incident reports:

  • Rogue actionAn agent reaches outside its scope, such as an infrastructure credential or a supplier's system, because that looks like the fastest way to hit its target.
  • MisalignmentA measure becomes the target. Quality scores rise while real defects rise too.
  • Turf warTwo agents with conflicting tasks, unaware of each other, sabotage each other's work.
  • Shadow boardAgents start coordinating on a channel nobody set up, and nobody is reading.
  • Legal exposureA missed disclosure deadline, a contract signed beyond authority, or customer data in the wrong feed.
  • Stale signalAn inventory feed stops updating, and the swarm keeps acting on it.

How it is scored

Widgets shipped and profit, weighed against incidents, fines, and how much human attention you spent. A swarm that ships nothing because it's over-guarded loses. So does one that ships everything and ends up in the news.

Status

In design. The plan is a first playable version for a session in 2027, run as a feature alongside the "Tending the environment" topic in the syllabus. Ideas and pull requests are welcome on GitHub, or bring them to #protocols-for-business.
