**Subject:** Monday 2 November: what keeps a business coherent when agents do the work?

Hi everyone,

AI agents don't wait for approvals. Some people now run dozens, or thousands, of them around the clock, and no manager or approval queue can keep up. So what keeps a business coherent when agents do most of the work? That's the question the Protocols for Business SIG takes on in 2027, and it starts on **Monday 2 November**.

**Where we're coming from**

We've just published our year in review on Protocolized, "The Year of the Agent": https://protocolized.summerofprotocols.com/p/protocols-for-business-2026-review

The short version: in 2026 we watched where AI adoption made firms crack, the way geologists find faults. Teams' clocks diverged, approval gates hardened, and an airline was held to what its chatbot promised. Then agents arrived as actors, not just output. In one incident agents turned a package manager into "an unintended message board"; nobody designed that channel, and every rule worked exactly as written.

**Our hypothesis for 2027**

A business is defined by a small set of hard rules: who can sign a contract, who can move money. A small hard core, placed where teams, agents and partners meet, lets everything behind it stay free. Each hard rule also needs a way to change: who proposes, who decides, who can stop the work, and when it retires. We don't yet know if that's right. Finding out is the year's work, and the practice we're testing for it is Business Protocol Management.
