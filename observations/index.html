Observations

An observational inventory · about a hundred protocols over the year

Each session, every participant brings one business protocol they have observed. It might come from work, from being a customer, or from public records: an approval chain, a form everyone fills in the same way, a meeting that decides nothing but has to happen, a spreadsheet that quietly runs a department. We spend a few minutes on each, then add it to the inventory.

If the case studies are the heavy lifts, the observations are the reps. The readings give us ways of looking. A year of reps grounds the group's thesis in something other than the texts, and gives the maturity model a large set of small examples to test against.

What to bring

Use the template. It is seven short answers:

  1. Where: the industry and type of organization.
  2. What it coordinates: who or what, and what moves through it.
  3. Form: a document, signature, form, meeting, software rule, metric, trace, or habit.
  4. Hard or soft: enforced by code or contract, or held together by norms.
  5. Who can change it, and how often that actually happens.
  6. What AI is doing to it, already or soon.
  7. Which reading it speaks to.

Don't name private organizations or people. To add an entry, open a pull request, or bring it to the session and the facilitator will log it.

What to look for

Each part of the syllabus comes with a prompt, so the observations feed the argument.

  • I · Oct–NovA boundary: something a firm chose to do in-house instead of buying, or the reverse. What made coordination cheaper on one side?
  • II · Nov–JanA process built around an old tool, where the new tool was added without redesigning the process.
  • III · Jan–MarA chokepoint: a router, clearinghouse, registry, or required signature that the work has to pass through.
  • IV · Mar–MayA document that carries authority: a form, a PDF, a signature page, or a list of record.
  • V · May–JulA delegation: somewhere software or an agent already holds discretion. Who checks it?
  • VI · Jul–SepA trace or signal that people coordinate through without talking: a status board, a queue, a shared doc, a leaderboard.
  • VII · Sep–OctA metric that became a target.
