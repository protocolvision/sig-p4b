Observations

Protocol watching · recorded with the Protocol Bicorder · about a hundred over the year

Each session, every participant brings one business protocol they have observed. It might come from work, from being a customer, or from public records: an approval chain, a form everyone fills in the same way, a meeting that decides nothing but has to happen, a spreadsheet that quietly runs a department. Each one gets a couple of minutes in the round-robin, then goes into the inventory.

If the case studies are the heavy lifts, the observations are the reps. The readings give us ways of looking. A year of reps grounds the group's thesis in something other than the texts, and gives the maturity model a large set of small examples to test against.

Open questions for protocol watching

You don't need a checklist to notice protocols. It helps to carry a question. These five come from The Unreasonable Sufficiency of Protocols in the Protocol Reader. Pick one and keep it in mind while you watch.

  1. "What is the relationship between protocols and agency?" This includes when the agent is software.
  2. "How do protocols mutate, and what are the limits on the mutability of a protocol beyond which it begins to lose coherence, identity, and utility?"
  3. "What makes a protocol legitimate?"
  4. "What are general principles of good protocol stewardship?"
  5. "Can protocols be immortal, and if not, what determines their natural lifespans?"

Recording with the Protocol Bicorder

We record observations with the Protocol Bicorder, a diagnostic tool for the study of protocols created by Nathan Schneider. For each pair of opposing terms, you place the protocol on a scale from 1 to 9. Examples include explicit / implicit, formal / vernacular, and sovereign / subsidiary. The pairs are grouped into Design, Entanglement, and Experience, and the Bicorder works out how hard or soft the protocol is. The short form takes a few minutes.

  1. Watch. Notice a protocol at work, as a customer, or in public records.
  2. Read it. Open the Bicorder and use the short form. In standpoint, say how you relate to the protocol.
  3. Add three notes. The industry and type of organization, what AI is doing to it, and which open question or reading it speaks to.
  4. File it. Export the JSON and add it to observations/ with a pull request, alongside a short note from the template. You can also bring it to the session and the facilitator will file it.

Don't name private organizations or people. The Bicorder also offers a public upload to its shared dataset. Use it only for protocols that are already public.
