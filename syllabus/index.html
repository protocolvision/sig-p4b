Protocols for Business SIG Syllabus Case studies

Syllabus

October 2026 – October 2027 · twenty-six sessions · one reading each

The syllabus follows the AI Native Data Operations project over a year, and each part builds on the one before. We start with the firm as a system for processing information, and look at how its structure, strategy, and processes have been redesigned around information before. Next come the documents and infrastructure that carry that information, and the end-to-end data pipeline of today's AI market, from datasets and models to evals and protocols such as MCP. Then the organization starts to look like a single cognitive system, whose members coordinate through a shared environment instead of by instruction. We end on emergent collaboration (stigmergy, signal broadcasting, and managing swarms in business settings), and on how to measure and improve it. The practical work runs separately, in the case studies.

Readings come from the Bret Victor reference library, current AI engineering research, classic texts on organization design, strategy, documents, and coordination, and the Summer of Protocols archive. We meet every other Monday, with a break over New Year. Each session starts with 15–20 minutes of silent reading and then 30–35 minutes of discussion. Where a text is long, we circulate an excerpt in advance.

Meetings & readings

I. The firm as an information system

  • 5 October 2026 The Nature of the Firm Coase, 1937 Why firms exist at all: coordinating inside a firm is sometimes cheaper than using the market. When AI changes what coordination costs, it changes where the edges of the firm fall.
  • 19 October 2026 The Use of Knowledge in Society Hayek, 1945 The knowledge an organization runs on is spread out, and nobody holds all of it. So the protocols that move it are where intervening pays off.
  • 2 November 2026 Organization Design: An Information Processing View Galbraith, 1974 The more uncertain the task, the more information an organization has to process. Structure is a set of choices about how to handle that load: rules, hierarchy, slack, lateral links, or information systems.
  • 16 November 2026 How Do Committees Invent? Conway, 1968 An organization builds systems that mirror how it communicates. If agents change who talks to whom, they change what gets built.

II. Information, strategy, and process

  • 30 November 2026 How Information Gives You Competitive Advantage Porter & Millar, 1985 Every activity in the value chain creates and uses information. When information technology changes, the links between activities change, and so does where the advantage lies.
  • 14 December 2026 Reengineering Work: Don't Automate, Obliterate Hammer, 1990 The last general-purpose technology wave: bolting computers onto old processes wasted them. The same trap, and the same opportunity, is in front of AI adoption now.
  • 11 January 2027 The New Industrial Engineering: Information Technology and Business Process Redesign Davenport & Short, 1990 The more methodical companion to Hammer: how IT and process redesign shape each other, and how to go about it step by step. It is the ancestor of the playbook we are writing.

III. Documents, infrastructure, and discovery

  • 25 January 2027 The Filing Cabinet Robertson, 2021 The 20th century's answer to spread-out knowledge: file it, and let the form carry what matters.
  • 8 February 2027 Visualisation and Cognition: Drawing Things Together Latour, 1986 Documents are immutable mobiles. They travel and pile up at centers of calculation. That is how a firm, a utility, or a public owner sees at a distance.
  • 22 February 2027 The Ethnography of Infrastructure Star, 1999 Plugs, standards, and forms. How to study infrastructure that is boring on purpose, and that nobody notices until it breaks.
  • 8 March 2027 As We May Think Bush, 1945 Filing breaks down at scale. Bush proposes finding things by association: trails through a record instead of an index into it. Discovery, not filing.

IV. The AI information pipeline

  • 22 March 2027 Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems Sculley et al., 2015 In a real ML system the model is a small box. Around it sit data collection, verification, feature extraction, configuration, and monitoring. Most of the cost, and most of the debt, lives in the data pipeline.
  • 5 April 2027 "Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work": Data Cascades in High-Stakes AI Sambasivan et al., 2021 Data cascades: small upstream problems in how data is collected and handled grow into failures downstream. Data work is undervalued, and that is where AI projects break.
  • 19 April 2027 Ecosystem Graphs: The Social Footprint of Foundation Models Bommasani et al., 2023 The closest thing we have to an end-to-end map of the AI market's information supply chain: datasets feed models, which feed applications, with dependencies at every step.
  • 3 May 2027 Model Context Protocol Specification modelcontextprotocol.io, 2024– The newest piece of the pipeline: an open protocol for connecting an organization's data and tools to models. It is where AI-native data operations meets protocol design.

V. People, machines, and shared cognition

  • 17 May 2027 Man-Computer Symbiosis Licklider, 1960 How work divides between people and machines: what can be specified in advance goes to the machine, what can't stays with people. This is the design rule for AI-native data operations.
  • 31 May 2027 How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds Hutchins, 1995 Memory and cognition live in people and artifacts together. Programs, agents, and documents now form one system, and this is a way to describe it.

VI. Coordination through the environment

  • 14 June 2027 A Brief History of Stigmergy Theraulaz & Bonabeau, 1999 Where the idea comes from: Grassé's termites, which coordinate by responding to what earlier work left behind, with no messages passed between them.
  • 28 June 2027 Stigmergy as a Universal Coordination Mechanism Heylighen, 2016 From insects to people and software. If a system's memory lives in its artifacts, its members can coordinate by leaving traces in a shared environment.
  • 12 July 2027 Securing Stigmergic Systems Nast, 2026 What goes wrong when agents coordinate through a shared environment: leaked traces, runaway feedback, and new attack surfaces.

VII. Signals and swarms

  • 26 July 2027 Flocks, Herds, and Schools: A Distributed Behavioral Model Reynolds, 1987 Three local rules produce flocking. There is no leader and no plan, only each member reacting to the signals of its neighbours.
  • 9 August 2027 The Swarm and the Formation Fernández, 2024 Human swarms act together without explicit protocols. Broadcast signals coordinate them, not instructions.
  • 23 August 2027 Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems Ostrom, 2010 Governing a swarm: many centers of decision, with rules made by the people who use them. Management shapes the conditions for coordination instead of directing it.

VIII. Measuring and bootstrapping

  • 6 September 2027 Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change Campbell, 1976 Campbell's law: the more a measure is used to make decisions, the more it gets distorted. How to evaluate emergent collaboration without the measure becoming the target.
  • 20 September 2027 Your AI Product Needs Evals Husain, 2024 Evals in practice: look at the data, write tests from the failures you see, and iterate. A measured system improves and an unmeasured one doesn't. This is the answer to Campbell in working form.
  • 4 October 2027 Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003 How a group gets better at getting better. This is where the maturity model has to end up.
