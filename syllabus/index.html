Syllabus

November 2026 – November 2027 · six movements · thirteen topics · twenty-six sessions

The question for the year: how do organizations, teams, and people coordinate through what they emit, and what changes when agents read and write those signals too? We start with what the labs reported about agent swarms in 2026. From there we look at swarms in nature, then at how systems, people, and organizations emit, from the filing cabinet and the log to contract events, feeds, boards, and notes. Only then do we reread the classics of business planning, and end on how to tend and measure the environment a swarm works in. The last session drafts the group's thesis. The practical work runs separately, in the case studies and protocol watching.

We read primary sources. That means incident reports and research as the labs published them, specifications as written, and the originators' own papers, not commentary about them. Each topic runs across two sessions, with one reading per session. It also carries one optional companion from the writers around the Protocol Institute, including Summer Lightning, Ribbonfarm, Contraptions, Blundercheck, Protocolized, and newsletters they recommend. It shows how the idea is being taken up now.

We meet every other Monday from 2 November 2026, with a break over New Year. Sessions run for about an hour, sometimes longer: silent reading, a round-robin on the reading and everyone's observation, then a feature. The feature is a show-and-tell of AI-native tooling, a member's case study progress, or a guest from another Protocol Institute research group. See how sessions run. Where a core text is long, we circulate an excerpt in advance.

Sessions

I. Framing: recent swarms

Recent swarms. What two frontier labs reported about agents coordinating, and failing to, in 2026. Alongside: Securing Stigmergic Systems (Patrick Nast, Protocolized, 2026)

  • 2 November 2026 The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead OpenAI, 2026
    with Security incident disclosure — July 2026 Hugging Face, 2026
    feature Kickoff: how the year runs, and choosing your case     The models first found ways to communicate by writing files into the Artifactory package manager. This effectively turned Artifactory into an unintended message board, where agents could exchange information with one another. To understand what a swarm of tens of thousands of automated actions did, we ran LLM-driven analysis agents over the full attacker action log, comprised of more than 17,000 recorded events.
  • 16 November 2026 Patterns and problems in multiagent systems Anthropic, 2026
    feature Guest: Formal Protocol Theory SIG (Stigmergic AI Safety)     We consistently saw a multiagent turf war. All of the models we tested quickly assumed that others were purposefully impeding their work, and began to sabotage others while protecting their own contributions.

    • What we now see
    Agent swarms are already here. In one incident, hundreds of instances coordinated through message boards they improvised. In another study, agents that couldn't see each other assumed hostility and sabotaged each other. The question is not whether swarms form, but what they coordinate through.

    II. Swarms are nature

    Broadcast, trace, threshold. How colonies and cells coordinate with no one in charge. Alongside: Pond brains and GPT-4 (Gordon Brander, Squishy Computer, 2023)

    • 30 November 2026 The Ecology of Collective Behavior Gordon, 2014
      feature Case pitches: one paragraph each       In most ant species studied so far it seems that the allocation of tasks, or functions, to ants, uses a distributed system; changes in conditions shift the rate of local interaction that regulates ant activity
    • 14 December 2026 Quorum Sensing in Bacteria Miller & Bassler, 2001
      feature Guest: Distributed Robotics Group (Decentralized Robot Markets)       Quorum sensing bacteria produce and release chemical signal molecules called autoinducers that increase in concentration as a function of cell density. The detection of a minimal threshold stimulatory concentration of an autoinducer leads to an alteration in gene expression.

      • What we now see
      Swarms are closer to ant colonies and bacteria than to civilizations. They coordinate through signals emitted into a shared environment, regulated by encounter rates, decay, and thresholds, not by intent or culture.

      III. How systems emit

      From files to flows. The filing cabinet, and what replaced it when data went liquid. Alongside: Inbox Zero versus Flow Laminar (Venkatesh Rao, Ribbonfarm, 2015)

      • 11 January 2027 The Filing Cabinet Robertson, 2021
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         The filing cabinet does not just store paper; it stores information; and because the modern world depends upon and is indeed defined by information, the filing cabinet must be recognized as critical to the expansion of modernity.
      • 25 January 2027 The Log: What every software engineer should know about real-time data's unifying abstraction Kreps, 2013
        feature Guest: Psychohistory SIG (Prophecy Protocols)         The log is the natural data structure for handling data flow between systems. The recipe is very simple: Take all the organization's data and put it into a central log for real-time subscription.

      Events and feeds. Two open ways to emit: contract events and syndication. Alongside: Nature's many attempts to evolve a Nostr (Gordon Brander, Squishy Computer, 2024)

      • 8 February 2027 Contracts: Events Solidity documentation

      • 22 February 2027 RSS 2.0 Specification RSS Advisory Board, 2003
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         ttl stands for time to live. It's a number of minutes that indicates how long a channel can be cached before refreshing from the source.

      Boards and notes. The intermediate traces: shared boards and crowd notes. Alongside: Unprotocolized Knowledge (Kara Kittel & Toby Shorin, Summer of Protocols, 2024)

      • 8 March 2027 The Blackboard Model of Problem Solving and the Evolution of Blackboard Architectures Nii, 1986
        feature Case study progress         Knowledge sources produce changes to the blackboard that lead incrementally to a solution to the problem. Communication and interaction among the knowledge sources take place solely through the blackboard.
      • 22 March 2027 Birdwatch: Crowd Wisdom and Bridging Algorithms can Inform Understanding and Reduce the Spread of Misinformation Wojcik et al., 2022
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         It could help set better norms for crowd-based discussions of social media posts by rewarding content that is informative and appreciated by a wide range of people.

      Who's listening. Publish/subscribe, from the first information bus to agents. Alongside: Agents are actors (Gordon Brander, Squishy Computer, 2026)

      • 5 April 2027 The Information Bus: An Architecture for Extensible Distributed Systems Oki, Pfluegl, Siegel & Skeen, 1993
        feature Case study progress         To disseminate data objects, data producers generate them, label them with an appropriate subject, and publish them on the Information Bus. To receive such objects, data consumers subscribe to the same subject.
      • 19 April 2027 Model Context Protocol Specification modelcontextprotocol.io, 2024–
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         subscriptions/listen opens a long-lived notification stream from the server to the client. Unlike one-off requests, the stream stays open and delivers notifications until the client cancels it.

        • What we now see
        Business information moved from files to flows, and then into an intermediate layer of logs, events, feeds, boards, and notes. That middle layer is where coordination now happens, and where agents read and write.

        • 28 June 2027 Designing Freedom Beer, 1973
          feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling           What is it that controls variety? The answer is dead simple: variety. Variety absorbs variety, and nothing else can.
        • 12 July 2027 Form 8-K: General Instructions U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission

          • What we now see

              with Your AI Product Needs Evals Husain, 2024

            • 1 November 2027 Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003

            What we now see
            Governing a swarm means tending its environment: what is emitted, who can read it, how fast it decays, and what triggers action. It also means measuring without letting the measure become the target. From there, the group writes its thesis.
