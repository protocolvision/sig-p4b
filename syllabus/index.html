Protocols for Business SIG Syllabus Case studies

Syllabus

October 2026 – March 2027 · twelve sessions · twelve readings

The syllabus follows the AI Native Data Operations project, and each part builds on the one before. We start with the claim that knowledge in an organization is spread out, and look at the documents and standards that have carried it. Then we ask what changes when machines can read everything. The organization starts to look like a single cognitive system, and its members can coordinate through a shared environment instead of by instruction. We end on emergent collaboration: signal broadcasting, stigmergy, and managing swarms in business settings. The practical work runs separately, in the case studies.

Readings come from the Bret Victor reference library, classic texts on knowledge, documents, and coordination, and the Summer of Protocols archive. We meet every other Monday. Each session starts with 15–20 minutes of silent reading and then 30–35 minutes of discussion. Where a text is long, we circulate an excerpt in advance.

Meetings & readings

I. Where knowledge lives

  • 5 October 2026 The Use of Knowledge in Society Hayek, 1945 Our starting claim: the knowledge an organization runs on is spread out, and nobody holds all of it. So the protocols that move it are where intervening pays off.
  • 19 October 2026 How Do Committees Invent? Conway, 1968 If knowledge is spread out, its channels shape everything else. An organization builds systems that mirror how it communicates.

II. The document layer

  • 2 November 2026 The Filing Cabinet Robertson, 2021 The 20th century's answer to spread-out knowledge: file it, and let the form carry what matters.
  • 16 November 2026 Visualisation and Cognition: Drawing Things Together Latour, 1986 Documents are immutable mobiles. They travel and pile up at centers of calculation. That is how a firm, a utility, or a public owner sees at a distance.

III. From filing to discovery

  • 30 November 2026 As We May Think Bush, 1945 Filing breaks down at scale. Bush proposes finding things by association: trails through a record instead of an index into it. Discovery, not filing.
  • 14 December 2026 Man-Computer Symbiosis Licklider, 1960 How work divides between people and machines: what can be specified in advance goes to the machine, what can't stays with people. This is the design rule for AI-native data operations.

IV. The organization as a cognitive system

  • 11 January 2027 How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds Hutchins, 1995 Memory and cognition live in people and artifacts together. Programs, agents, and documents now form one system, and this is a way to describe it.

V. Coordination through the environment

  • 25 January 2027 Stigmergy as a Universal Coordination Mechanism Heylighen, 2016 If the system's memory lives in its artifacts, agents can coordinate by leaving traces in a shared environment instead of messaging each other.
  • 8 February 2027 Securing Stigmergic Systems Nast, 2026 What goes wrong when agents coordinate through a shared environment: leaked traces, runaway feedback, and new attack surfaces.

VI. Signals and swarms

  • 22 February 2027 The Swarm and the Formation Fernández, 2024 Swarms act together without explicit protocols. Broadcast signals coordinate them, not instructions.
  • 8 March 2027 Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems Ostrom, 2010 Governing a swarm: many centers of decision, with rules made by the people who use them. Management shapes the conditions for coordination instead of directing it.

VII. Bootstrapping

  • 22 March 2027 Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003 How a group gets better at getting better. This is where the maturity model has to end up.
