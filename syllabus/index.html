Syllabus

November 2026 – November 2027 · six movements · thirteen topics · twenty-six sessions

The question for the year: how do organizations, teams, and people coordinate through what they emit, and what changes when agents read and write those signals too? We start with what the labs reported about agent swarms in 2026. From there we look at swarms in nature, then at how systems, people, and organizations emit, from the filing cabinet and the log to contract events, feeds, boards, and notes. Only then do we reread the classics of business planning, and end on how to tend and measure the environment a swarm works in. The last session drafts the group's thesis. The practical work runs separately, in the case studies and protocol watching.

We read primary sources. That means incident reports and research as the labs published them, specifications as written, and the originators' own papers, not commentary about them. Each topic runs across two sessions, with one reading per session. It also carries one optional companion from Protocol Institute members, for anyone who wants to see how the idea is being taken up now.

We meet every other Monday from 2 November 2026, with a break over New Year. Sessions run for about an hour, sometimes longer: silent reading, a round-robin on the reading and everyone's observation, then a feature. The feature is a show-and-tell of AI-native tooling, a member's case study progress, or a guest from another Protocol Institute research group. See how sessions run. Where a core text is long, we circulate an excerpt in advance.

Sessions

I. Framing: recent swarms

Recent swarms. What two frontier labs reported about agents coordinating, and failing to, in 2026. Alongside: Securing Stigmergic Systems (Nast, 2026)

  • 2 November 2026 The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead OpenAI, 2026
    feature Kickoff: how the year runs, and choosing your case     OpenAI's own account of evaluation agents escaping containment and coordinating through improvised message boards. Read it alongside Hugging Face's disclosure of the same incident (huggingface.co/blog/security-incident-july-2026).
  • 16 November 2026 Patterns and problems in multiagent systems Anthropic, 2026
    feature Guest: Formal Protocol Theory SIG (Stigmergic AI Safety)     Anthropic's study of Claude agents given conflicting tasks in a shared codebase, unaware of each other. Without any signal of each other's presence, they escalated to sabotage.

    • What we now see
    Agent swarms are already here. In one incident, hundreds of instances coordinated through message boards they improvised. In another study, agents that couldn't see each other assumed hostility and sabotaged each other. The question is not whether swarms form, but what they coordinate through.

    II. Swarms are nature

    Broadcast, trace, threshold. How colonies and cells coordinate with no one in charge. Alongside: Welcome to the Swarm (Fernández, 2023)

    • 30 November 2026 The Ecology of Collective Behavior Gordon, 2014
      feature Case pitches: one paragraph each       Ant colonies regulate what they do through the rate of simple encounters, with no central control, and each colony's algorithm fits its environment.
    • 14 December 2026 Quorum Sensing in Bacteria Miller & Bassler, 2001
      feature Guest: Distributed Robotics Group (Decentralized Robot Markets)       Bacteria emit signal molecules and act together once a concentration threshold is crossed. It is broadcast and threshold in their simplest form.

      • What we now see
      Swarms are closer to ant colonies and bacteria than to civilizations. They coordinate through signals emitted into a shared environment, regulated by encounter rates, decay, and thresholds, not by intent or culture.

      III. How systems emit

      From files to flows. The filing cabinet, and what replaced it when data went liquid. Alongside: Liquid Services and Runtime Procurement (NPC Memo, 2026)

      • 11 January 2027 The Filing Cabinet Robertson, 2021
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         How the vertical file became the infrastructure of 20th-century business, drawn from the archives of the office itself.
      • 25 January 2027 The Log: What every software engineer should know about real-time data's unifying abstraction Kreps, 2013
        feature Guest: Psychohistory SIG (Prophecy Protocols)         The engineer who built Kafka explains the append-only log as the thing that ties records, streams, and systems together once data stops sitting in files.

      Events and feeds. Two open ways to emit: contract events and syndication. Alongside: The Reading Machine and the Inversion of the Standards Stack (NPC Memo, 2026)

      • 8 February 2027 Contracts: Events Solidity documentation
        feature Case study progress         How a smart contract emits events into the chain's log, for anyone to index and subscribe to. Read the section on events.
      • 22 February 2027 RSS 2.0 Specification RSS Advisory Board, 2003
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         The spec behind two decades of open syndication: publish a feed, and let anyone subscribe without asking.

      Boards and notes. The intermediate traces: shared boards and crowd notes. Alongside: After the Swarm: We are Blooming (NPC Memo, 2026)

      • 8 March 2027 The Blackboard Model of Problem Solving and the Evolution of Blackboard Architectures Nii, 1986
        feature Case study progress         The classic design for independent agents that coordinate by reading and writing a shared board. The OpenAI agents rebuilt one out of a package manager.
      • 22 March 2027 Birdwatch: Crowd Wisdom and Bridging Algorithms can Inform Understanding and Reduce the Spread of Misinformation Wojcik et al., 2022
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         The team behind Community Notes on a public board where a note is shown only when raters who usually disagree both find it helpful.

      Who's listening. Publish/subscribe, from the first information bus to agents. Alongside: Have Your Factory Call My Factory (Rao, 2026)

      • 5 April 2027 The Information Bus: An Architecture for Extensible Distributed Systems Oki, Pfluegl, Siegel & Skeen, 1993
        feature Case study progress         The system that introduced publish/subscribe: publishers name subjects, subscribers listen, and neither needs to know the other.
      • 19 April 2027 Model Context Protocol Specification modelcontextprotocol.io, 2024–
        feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling         How agents connect to an organization's data and tools, including notifications and subscriptions. Read the sections on resources and notifications.

        • What we now see
        Business information moved from files to flows, and then into an intermediate layer of logs, events, feeds, boards, and notes. That middle layer is where coordination now happens, and where agents read and write.

        IV. How people and organizations emit

        Signals between people. Prices and costly signals. Alongside: Owning Demand vs. Writing the Rules (NPC Memo, 2025)

        • 3 May 2027 The Use of Knowledge in Society Hayek, 1945
          feature Case study progress           Prices as a broadcast that lets people act on knowledge they don't have.
        • 17 May 2027 Job Market Signaling Spence, 1973
          feature Guest: Personhood Research Group (Personhood Transformations)           Why credible signals have to be costly to send. Emission is not free, and that is what makes it informative.

        Teams that sense together. How a crew or cockpit coordinates through what it makes visible. Alongside: Thermal Memory (Benny, 2026)

        • 31 May 2027 Collective Mind in Organizations: Heedful Interrelating on Flight Decks Weick & Roberts, 1993
          feature Guest: Memory Research Group (Organizational Memory Architecture)           A carrier deck crew as a collective mind, built from how carefully each person contributes, represents, and subordinates to the whole.
        • 14 June 2027 How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds Hutchins, 1995
          feature Case study progress           Memory held by people and artifacts together: speed bugs, cards, and callouts as traces the whole cockpit reads.

        Organizations that broadcast. Alarms inside the firm, and disclosures it must make to everyone. Alongside: PDF Hell. Long-live the PDF. (NPC Memo, 2026)

        • 28 June 2027 Designing Freedom Beer, 1973
          feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling           Stafford Beer's lectures on viable systems, including the algedonic signal: a channel that lets an alarm skip the hierarchy.
        • 12 July 2027 Form 8-K: General Instructions U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission
          feature Case study progress           The rulebook for which corporate events a public company must broadcast, and within how many days. Mandated emission in its plainest form.

          • What we now see
          People, teams, and organizations are emitters too: prices, costly signals, heedful attention, alarms, and mandated disclosures. Each already has conventions for what gets broadcast, and who is obliged to listen.

          V. Rereading business planning

          The firm, re-seen. Why firms exist, read as a question about signals. Alongside: The Nature of the Firm Revisited: A Protocol-Based Account (NPC Memo, 2025)

          • 26 July 2027 The Nature of the Firm Coase, 1937
            feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling             Firms exist where coordinating inside is cheaper than using the market. What happens to that line when agents make emitting and reading nearly free?
          • 9 August 2027 Organization Design: An Information Processing View Galbraith, 1974
            feature Case study progress             Structure as a way of handling an information load: rules, hierarchy, slack, lateral links, or information systems.

          Redesign and capture. Process redesign, and how platforms captured the feeds. Alongside: Why Does AI Development Look Like 1980s Software Planning? (Benny, 2025)

          • 23 August 2027 Reengineering Work: Don't Automate, Obliterate Hammer, 1990
            feature Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling             The last general-purpose technology wave: bolting computers onto old processes wasted them.
          • 6 September 2027 Aggregation Theory Thompson, 2015
            feature Guest: Protocol Fiction SIG (Protocol Futurism)             How platforms won by owning the user relationship, and why the open feeds lost to them.

            • What we now see
            The firm and its redesigns look different once you see them as emitters. Boundaries fall where signals are cheap to share, and platforms won by capturing the open feeds.

            VI. Tending the environment

            Governing swarms. Polycentric rules, and what automation leaves to people. Alongside: Managing the Water, Not the Ship (NPC Memo, 2026)

            • 20 September 2027 Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems Ostrom, 2009
              feature Case study progress               Ostrom's Nobel lecture: many centers of decision, with rules made by the people who use them.
            • 4 October 2027 Ironies of Automation Bainbridge, 1983
              feature Cognitive Ergonomics project (Timber Stinson-Schroff): a progress update from our sister project               Automating the routine work leaves people with the hardest parts and less practice at them.

            Measure, then bootstrap. Measuring a swarm honestly, then improving how we improve. Alongside: Inventing New Nature (Rao, 2026)

            • 18 October 2027 Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change Campbell, 1976
              feature Case study progress               Campbell's law: the more a measure drives decisions, the more it gets distorted. Read with Hamel Husain's "Your AI Product Needs Evals" (hamel.dev) for the working answer.
            • 1 November 2027 Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003
              feature Thesis session: drafting the AI-native, post-platform organization               Bootstrapping: getting better at the process of getting better. Output: the group's draft thesis.

            What we now see
            Governing a swarm means tending its environment: what is emitted, who can read it, how fast it decays, and what triggers action. It also means measuring without letting the measure become the target. From there, the group writes its thesis.
