Protocols for Business SIG Syllabus Case studies

Syllabus

October 2026 – March 2027 · twelve sessions · twenty readings

These are the readings behind the AI Native Data Operations project. They cover how organizations know things, how information gets filed, standardized, and passed around, and what happens to all of that once machines can read any document. The practical work runs separately, in the case studies.

Readings come from the Bret Victor reference library, the Summer of Protocols and Protocolized archives, and our own notes. We meet every other Monday. Each session starts with 15–20 minutes of silent reading and then 30–35 minutes of discussion. Where a text is long, we circulate an excerpt in advance.

Meetings & readings

  • 5 October 2026 The Use of Knowledge in Society Hayek, 1945
    Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System Meadows, 1999     Knowledge is spread across an organization, and some interventions do far more than others. Where do data operations sit on Meadows's list?
  • 19 October 2026 A Typology of Organisational Cultures Westrum, 2004
    How Do Committees Invent? Conway, 1968     An organization's culture shows in how information moves through it. What it builds mirrors how it communicates.
  • 2 November 2026 The Immaturity of CMM Bach, 1994
    Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change Campbell, 1976     The case against maturity models, and Campbell's law. We read these before revising our own model.
  • 16 November 2026 The Filing Cabinet Robertson, 2021
    The Reading Machine and the Inversion of the Standards Stack Fernández, 2026     A century of organizing information by filing it. What changes when a machine can read anything, so the hard part becomes finding it?
  • 30 November 2026 A World of Standards but not a Standard World Timmermans & Epstein, 2010
    OSI: The Internet That Wasn't Russell, 2013     How standards actually get made, and a well-known standard that lost to rough consensus and running code.
  • 14 December 2026 What is a "Document"? Buckland, 1997
    PDF Hell. Long-live the PDF. Fernández, 2026     What counts as a document is a matter of function, not format. Why the PDF is still the unit that business runs on.
  • 11 January 2027 Visualisation and Cognition: Drawing Things Together Latour, 1986 Immutable mobiles: how inscriptions travel and pile up at centers of calculation. What in a bid package or a rate sheet plays that role?
  • 25 January 2027 End-to-End Arguments in System Design Saltzer, Reed & Clark, 1984
    Adversarial Interoperability Doctorow, 2019     Where should a function live in a network? And interoperability you don't have to ask permission for.
  • 8 February 2027 The Ethnography of Infrastructure Star, 1999 Plugs, standards, and forms. How to study infrastructure that is boring on purpose.
  • 22 February 2027 How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds Hutchins, 1995 Memory is spread across people and their artifacts. It gives us a way to describe a data operation as a single cognitive system.
  • 8 March 2027 Programming as Theory Building Naur, 1985 A program is a theory held by the people who wrote it. What has to be written down for a playbook to still work after its authors leave?
  • 22 March 2027 The Computer as a Communication Device Licklider & Taylor, 1968
    Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003     Two early visions of networked organizations, and bootstrapping: getting better at the process of getting better.
