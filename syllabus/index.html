Protocols for Business SIG Syllabus Observations Case studies

Syllabus

October 2026 – October 2027 · twenty-six sessions · a core text and a recent discussion in each

The question for the year is what an AI-native, post-platform organization is. The syllabus works toward an answer one step at a time. It moves from why firms exist, through the scale paradigm and its redesigns, the end of the platform era, and the AI industry's information networks, to agents and swarms as full members of an organization. Each part ends with a claim that the next part has to build on. The last session drafts the group's thesis. The practical work runs separately, in the case studies.

Each session pairs a core text, a classic of economics, management, or systems thinking, with a recent discussion from Protocol Institute members that takes the question up under AI. These come from NPC Memo, Sachin Benny's Archival Time series, and Venkatesh Rao's essays in Protocolized and Contraptions. Everyone also brings one observed business protocol to each session. Each part has its own prompt for what to look for.

We meet every other Monday, with a break over New Year. Each session starts with 15–20 minutes of silent reading, then 30–35 minutes of discussion, then a short round of observations. Where a core text is long, we circulate an excerpt in advance.

Meetings & readings

I. Why firms exist

  • 5 October 2026 The Nature of the Firm Coase, 1937
    from PI members The Nature of the Firm Revisited: A Protocol-Based Account NPC Memo, 2025     Coase asks why firms exist at all. The response restates his answer in protocol terms: the boundary falls where management and production protocols are cheaper than transaction protocols.
  • 19 October 2026 The Use of Knowledge in Society Hayek, 1945
    from PI members The Unreasonable Sufficiency of Protocols Rao et al., 2023     Hayek shows that knowledge is spread out and that prices coordinate it. Rao shows that compact protocols coordinate far more than we expect.
  • 2 November 2026 Organization Design: An Information Processing View Galbraith, 1974
    from PI members Why Does AI Development Look Like 1980s Software Planning? Benny, 2025     Galbraith treats structure as a way to handle an information load. Benny shows that AI flips which coordination is cheap, and pushes firms back toward frozen, shared definitions.

claim carried forward
The firm is a bundle of coordination protocols. Its boundary sits where internal protocols are cheaper than market ones. AI reprices them: documentation becomes nearly free, and drift in shared meaning becomes expensive.

II. Scale and its redesigns

  • 16 November 2026 The Visible Hand: The Managerial Revolution in American Business (introduction) Chandler, 1977
    from PI members Beyond Szabo Scaling Rao, 2025     The managerial hierarchy as a coordination technology, and the argument that it maximized manageability over expressivity. By this account we are still at the mainframe stage of social scaling.
  • 30 November 2026 How Information Gives You Competitive Advantage Porter & Millar, 1985
    from PI members Owning Demand vs. Writing the Rules NPC Memo, 2025     Information as competitive advantage in the value chain, and what came next: aggregators capture the surplus while protocols hold the rule-making power.
  • 14 December 2026 Reengineering Work: Don't Automate, Obliterate Hammer, 1990
    from PI members Management as Protocol: The Subsumption of Managerial Work NPC Memo, 2025     BPR redesigns the process once, from the top. The response traces what happened next: management stopped writing protocols and came to live inside software that executes them.
  • 11 January 2027 The Dynamo and the Computer David, 1990
    from PI members General-Purpose Technologies as Annealing Agents Benny, 2026     Why new technologies take decades to pay off, and a thermal model of the same lag. Institutions are heated, their dislocations move, and then they are pinned again.

claim carried forward
Scale organizations were built to be manageable, not expressive. Strategy and process redesign rebuilt them around the last general-purpose technology. Each such technology first heats institutions until old protocols stop binding, and then new protocols pin them into shape. AI is a new heating.

III. After the platform

  • 25 January 2027 How Do Committees Invent? Conway, 1968
    from PI members Protocol Review 1: Multisig Signing NPC Memo, 2025     Systems mirror communication structures. The update: the org chart flows out of who has to sign, including thresholds of M humans and K agents.
  • 8 February 2027 Aggregation Theory Thompson, 2015
    from PI members Liquid Services and Runtime Procurement NPC Memo, 2026     The platform era's own theory of power, and what replaces it when agents procure services at runtime and whoever couples demand to settlement holds the power.
  • 22 February 2027 Ecosystem Graphs: The Social Footprint of Foundation Models Bommasani et al., 2023
    from PI members The Fabric and the Brain Rao, 2026     A map of the AI industry's supply chain from datasets to models to applications, read against a post-platform picture: AI as the brain, protocols as the fabric.
  • 8 March 2027 Model Context Protocol Specification modelcontextprotocol.io, 2024–
    from PI members Have Your Factory Call My Factory Rao, 2026     The protocol that now connects organizations' data and tools to models, and the hidden bulk of the agentic iceberg: high-trust links between agent factories.

claim carried forward
In the AI industry's information networks, power moves away from destinations that aggregate demand. It moves to routing, clearing, and trusted links between agent factories. The org chart follows the signature graph.

IV. Memory, documents, and discovery

  • 22 March 2027 The Filing Cabinet Robertson, 2021
    from PI members PDF Hell. Long-live the PDF. NPC Memo, 2026     The 20th century filed its knowledge. Now machines do the reading and looking, the official work stays human, and the signature moves from the page to the list.
  • 5 April 2027 As We May Think Bush, 1945
    from PI members The Reading Machine and the Inversion of the Standards Stack NPC Memo, 2026     Finding by association instead of filing. When machines can read anything, the unit of standardization moves from the document to the pointer, and then to the proof.
  • 19 April 2027 How a Cockpit Remembers Its Speeds Hutchins, 1995
    from PI members Knowledge Chronotopes Benny, 2025     Memory is spread across people and their artifacts. Models, by contrast, have no sense of time, so they need landmark memory, much as common law relies on precedent.
  • 3 May 2027 Pace Layering: How Complex Systems Learn and Keep Learning Brand, 1999
    from PI members Thermal Memory Benny, 2026     The fast layers learn and the slow layers remember. Every general-purpose technology rewires memory at each layer, and models trained on a commons can end up draining it.
  • 17 May 2027 The Ethnography of Infrastructure Star, 1999
    from PI members Theorizing Protocolization I: New Nature Rao & Nast, 2026     Infrastructure is boring by design, and protocolization is progress through invisibility. The cost is overprotocolization and tangles that nobody governs.

claim carried forward
An organization remembers through documents and artifacts spread across pace layers. Once reading is free, value moves to pointers, provenance, landmarks, and signatures. Infrastructure that works becomes invisible, and so it has to be governed on purpose.

V. Agents as members

  • 31 May 2027 Man-Computer Symbiosis Licklider, 1960
    from PI members What is an Agent is not an Instrument NPC Memo, 2026     Licklider divides the work between people and machines. The response adds the variable that decides it: discretion. An agent combines orientation, initiative, authority to execute, and independence in time.
  • 14 June 2027 Theory of the Firm: Managerial Behavior, Agency Costs and Ownership Structure Jensen & Meckling, 1976
    from PI members Agent-Shaped Problems NPC Memo, 2026     Principals, agents, and the cost of monitoring, now with literal agents. A problem is agent-shaped only in its residue, and delegating it needs cheap verification and a hard gate.
  • 28 June 2027 Ironies of Automation Bainbridge, 1983
    from PI members Infrastructure of Firm Control NPC Memo, 2026     Automating the routine work leaves people with the hardest parts and less practice at them. The same irony reaches the board: it keeps formal authority but loses real control unless that control becomes executable.
  • 12 July 2027 The Architecture of Complexity Simon, 1962
    from PI members Our Eukaryotic Moment Rao, 2026     Complex systems are built from stable, nearly independent parts. The response pictures the AI-human organization as a new kind of cell, with AI as the nucleus and humans as the mitochondria.

claim carried forward
An agent becomes a member of the organization when it holds delegated discretion. Delegation has to be explicit and checkable, and enforced as executable constraints. Otherwise control drains away while authority stays on paper, and people lose the skill to check the work.

VI. Stigmergy, signals, and swarms

  • 26 July 2027 Stigmergy as a Universal Coordination Mechanism Heylighen, 2016
    from PI members Securing Stigmergic Systems Nast, 2026     Coordinating by leaving traces in a shared environment, from termites to wikis to agents. Then what goes wrong: leaked traces, runaway feedback, and new attack surfaces.
  • 9 August 2027 Flocks, Herds, and Schools: A Distributed Behavioral Model Reynolds, 1987
    from PI members Welcome to the Swarm Fernández, 2023     Three local rules produce a flock. Human and agent swarms are coordinated by broadcast signals and algorithmic feedback, with almost no explicit protocol.
  • 23 August 2027 Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems Ostrom, 2010
    from PI members Managing the Water, Not the Ship NPC Memo, 2026     Many centers of decision, with rules made by the people who use them. Some systems need captains and others need gardeners.
  • 6 September 2027 An Introduction to Cybernetics, ch. 11: Requisite Variety Ashby, 1956
    from PI members After the Swarm: We are Blooming NPC Memo, 2026     Only variety can absorb variety. A shared model substrate produces blooms, where people converge on the same thinking without ever being in contact, and tools built for swarms can't see them.

claim carried forward
Coordination moves into the shared environment. Members follow traces and signals, not instructions. Management becomes tending the conditions, like gardening or forestry, and the main risk is convergence, where the whole system loses variety.

VII. Measuring and bootstrapping

  • 20 September 2027 Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change Campbell, 1976
    from PI members Your AI Product Needs Evals Husain, 2024     Campbell's law: the more a measure drives decisions, the more it gets distorted. The working answer is to look at the data, write tests from the failures you see, and iterate. This is how the maturity model gets validated.
  • 4 October 2027 Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003
    from PI members Inventing New Nature Rao, 2026     Bootstrapping, set beside the Protocol Institute's own account of running as an AI-native research institution. Output: the group's draft thesis.

claim carried forward
Measure the organization with evals built from real failures, and keep the measure from becoming the target. Then write the thesis of the AI-native, post-platform organization, and keep improving it.
