Research

Our 2027 focus is AI Native Data Operations, meaning how organizations coordinate when much of the work is done by people working through AI and by agents talking to agents. We use it to mature Business Protocol Management, the SIG’s method for building a hard core of protocols into a company’s architecture and running people and agents inside it.

Premises

The research rests on three premises. We treat them as observations that could turn out partial or wrong, and the sessions and cases test them.

  • Abundant cognition

    Reading and writing both get cheap. Processing unstructured data (PDFs, rate sheets, forms) and producing software cost a fraction of what they did.

    Tested in the water and construction cases

  • Distributed agency

    Models act, and they run as many instances at once. Swarms of agents show up wherever work is done.

    Read against OpenAI’s incident report and Anthropic’s multi-agent study in theme I

  • Mediation

    Information increasingly flows through models: people and agents find, read, and exchange it by way of LLMs and the protocols that connect them.

    Read against the Model Context Protocol in theme I and the Agent Payments Protocol in theme V

What we study

If those premises hold, running data operations means managing a swarm through its protocols. We study the three phases of Business Protocol Management.

  • See

    Seeing the protocols a business actually runs on, which usually go unnoticed until they break. We start with field logs, where the work records what happens and why as it happens, so recurring patterns become visible. What an organization emits, who can read it and how long it stays current shows where its protocols live. Nature helps us see too, because cells and flocks coordinate through signals and thresholds their neighbours can read.

    Themes II, III and IV

  • Design

    What agents can see and do, and the hard core that bounds them: permissions, data contracts, spending limits, and the checks every output must pass. That includes scoring agents against real outcomes and versioning their models, prompts and context.

    Themes I and V

  • Evolve

    How discretion moves between people and agents, how it gets checked cheaply and recorded where others can see it, and how the hard core gets amended as the business grows.

    Theme VI

Open questions

These need research and partners. If you can help with one, get in touch.

  1. Which few protocols should a company make hard, and how much freedom can it allow inside them? Companies rebuilding part of their business around AI, software architects, organization researchers.
  2. How do we measure growth and prevented failures together? Reliability teams that track near-misses and delivery speed.
  3. Where should decisions sit between people and agents, and how do we check them cheaply? Teams that run agents in production.
  4. When should a free pattern become part of the hard core, and when should a hard rule soften? Platform teams, standards bodies, institutional designers.
  5. What should a company publish, to whom, and how long should it stay current? Public agencies, data publishers, companies that share operational data.
