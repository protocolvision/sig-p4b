# E-Trading Risk & Control

Covers: E-Trading Risk & Control (ETRC), Electronic (and Algorithmic) Trading Risk Manager, Algo Risk & Control, Electronic Trading Controls roles at banks, brokers and trading firms.

Collection method: web search results only (no pages opened). Every item is graded **S** (search summary only). Quotation marks mark words that appeared in a search result; everything else is paraphrase.

## Status

The regulatory basis for the role appears in this evidence in 2010. On 3 November 2010 the SEC adopted Rule 15c3-5 (the Market Access Rule), with compliance from 14 July 2011. It requires broker-dealers with market access to run automated pre-trade financial and regulatory controls under their "direct and exclusive control", to review those controls regularly, and to have the CEO certify them every year. FIA's industry work on risk controls follows: a 2013-era comment letter called kill switches "an effective last-resort means of risk control", and FIA consolidated its best practices in July 2024. In Europe and the UK, MiFID II's RTS 6 (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589) sets staffing and compliance-function requirements. The FCA's February 2018 multi-firm review found some firms "lacked a robust and comprehensive governance framework". PRA SS5/18 (effective 30 June 2018) asks firms to assign responsibility for approving algorithms, owning algorithm and risk-control inventories, owning kill switches and reviewing incidents. As a named job, the role appears in the evidence in bank postings from late 2024 to 2026 (NatWest, Citi, Nomura, BMO). Those postings describe a team inside the front office or Markets COO, usually first line of defence, with a separate Market Risk version at Nomura. It is an established specialist function at large dealers. Titles vary (ETRC, EAT Risk, Algo Risk & Control), and years of experience are typically 6–10 for VP. Sources: SEC release 34-63241; FIA 2024 paper; RTS 6 text; FCA 2018 review; PRA SS5/18; postings below (all S).

## Capability models

### EU RTS 6: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/589, Articles 2 and 3 (organisational requirements for algorithmic trading)
- Owner: European Commission (under MiFID II); retained in UK law
- Year: 2017 (published); applicable with MiFID II from 2018
- URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/589/article/2 ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/589/article/3/data.htm ; EU text mirror https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32017R0589
- Grade: S
- Competence requirements as listed:
  - Article 2: compliance staff have at least a general understanding of how the firm's algorithmic trading systems and algorithms work. They stay in regular contact with staff who have detailed technical knowledge, and in ongoing contact with those who can use the kill functionality (Art. 12) or who run each system or algorithm.
  - Article 3: enough staff with the skills to manage the algorithmic systems, with technical knowledge of (a) the systems and algorithms, (b) their monitoring and testing, (c) the trading strategies deployed, and (d) the firm's legal obligations. The firm specifies the skills required. Staff hold them at recruitment or gain them through training, and the firm keeps them current and assesses them regularly. The rest of paragraph 3 was cut off in the result.

### PRA Supervisory Statement SS5/18, Algorithmic trading
- Owner: Prudential Regulation Authority (Bank of England)
- Year: June 2018; effective 30 June 2018 (PRA Rulebook version dated 04-10-2025 also listed)
- URL: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2018/algorithmic-trading-ss ; PDF https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/supervisory-statement/2018/ss518.pdf
- Grade: S
- Expectations as listed:
  - The governing body approves the governance framework, which assigns responsibility for reviewing and approving algorithms, owning the algorithm and risk-control inventories, owning kill-switch controls, and running a review process for incidents where an algorithm or risk control fails.
  - Responsibility for algorithmic trading is mapped to a Senior Management Function.
  - Traders understand algorithms, venues and liquidity before trading electronically.
  - Trader access to algorithms matches remit and risk appetite.
  - Each function with a role in approving algorithms (front office, risk, other control functions) signs off the risks relevant to it.
  - The statement has sections on the algorithm approval process and on risk management and other control functions (detail not retrieved).

### FIA, Best Practices for Automated Trading Risk Controls and System Safeguards
- Owner: FIA (Futures Industry Association)
- Year: July 2024 (consolidates earlier FIA work from the 2010s)
- URL: https://fia.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/FIA_WP_AUTOMATED%20TRADING%20RISK%20CONTROLS_FINAL_0.pdf ; announcement https://www.fia.org/fia/articles/fia-releases-best-practices-automated-trading-risk-controls-and-system-safeguards
- Grade: S
- Areas:
  - Pre-trade risk management: maximum order size, maximum intraday position, price tolerance, cancel-on-disconnect, kill switches.
  - Exchange volatility control mechanisms.
  - Post-trade analysis.
  - Exchange-based conformance testing.
  - Earlier FIA principle: all users and providers of e-trading systems "have a responsibility to implement pre-trade risk controls appropriate to their role in the market".

### SEC Rule 15c3-5, Market Access Rule
- Owner: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission
- Year: adopted 3 November 2010; compliance 14 July 2011
- URL: https://www.SEC.gov/rules/final/2010/34-63241.pdf ; compliance guide https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2010/34-63241-secg.htm
- Grade: S
- Required controls as listed:
  - Automated pre-trade controls that keep customer activity within credit and capital thresholds.
  - Blocking of erroneous or duplicative orders.
  - Enforcement of regulatory requirements.
  - Controls under the broker-dealer's "direct and exclusive control".
  - Regular review of effectiveness, with prompt fixing of issues.
  - Annual CEO certification.
  - No "one-size-fits-all" standard.

### FCA, Algorithmic Trading Compliance in Wholesale Markets (multi-firm review)
- Owner: Financial Conduct Authority
- Year: February 2018
- URL: https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/multi-firm-reviews/algorithmic-trading-compliance-wholesale-markets.pdf
- Grade: S
- Areas:
  - Defining algorithmic trading.
  - Development and testing.
  - Risk controls.
  - Governance and oversight.
  - Market conduct.
- Findings captured: documentation of development and testing needed improvement; some firms "lacked a robust and comprehensive governance framework".
