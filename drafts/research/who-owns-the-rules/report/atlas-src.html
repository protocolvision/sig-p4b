The agent controls atlas · October 2026 · public documentation

What rules can firms set on AI agents?

We read the public documentation of 46 agent products and protocols, from 31 vendors, and listed every control they offer. We sorted the controls into one map, set them beside the controls of trading, payments, cloud, email, IT change, industrial safety and ordinary enterprise software, and dated how they arrived since 2021. Firms can now set many rules on what agents see, say and touch. The controls that limit what agents do in total, or cancel everything they are doing at once, are missing, and no law in force requires them yet. This report accompanies Who owns the rules.

The short version

Firms can set many rules on what an agent sees, says and touches; the controls that limit agents in total are missing, and no law in force asks for them yet

Products offer controls on content, tools, identity, data, spend and records. Most are optional settings. Compared with automated trading, agents lack the controls that bound a whole system: cancelling every agent’s in-flight actions at once, a limit on what all agents together can move, and value caps outside payments. The newest controls act on agent tools and runs, not only on text. Below are our ten safest findings, most certain first.

A control is one documented setting, policy or feature that constrains, changes, approves or records what an AI model or agent may do. One vocabulary marks how sure we are: High, the evidence shows; Medium, it points to; Low, an early sign.

    Explainer · How agent controls differ

    The codes are judgements from documentation, made by two coders and settled by a third. The two sides use different units: 83 control types in five kinds of enterprise software, and 14 categories of agent control.

    Ordinary software controls are mostly fixed rules (72 of 83 types); agent controls mostly sit around a model whose behaviour is not written in code, and their classifiers cluster in content and prompt-attack checks

    Firms already control access to apps, duties in ERP systems, software robots, APIs and feature releases. We coded 83 of those control types, and the 14 agent categories, on the same three questions. One square is one control type; the larger squares are the agent categories.

          Blind spots

          Absence rests on 2,046 documented controls, text searches of the inventory and 303 dated events. A control a vendor offers but does not document would be missed. “Announced work” means a dated event or documented control, not stated intent.

          The blind spots are controls that bound agents in total: no vendor documents cancelling every agent’s in-flight actions, a firm-wide limit or a value cap outside payments

          Ranked first by whether the control is required in its home domain, then by what goes wrong without it. Because trading is the only domain where we collected binding law, trading controls rank high by construction. Only one, guardrail independence, has announced work beyond protocol sign-in.

          1. Cancelling the in-flight actions of all of a firm’s agents at once

            • Law in trading (RTS 6); EU AI Act stop button from Dec 2027
            • Announced work: none

            Trading firms must be able to cancel “any or all of its unexecuted orders” at once. Agent off switches exist: Microsoft Entra disables one agent or every agent from one blueprint, which stops sign-ins; ServiceNow can pause traffic across all MCP servers and deactivate agents; MCP and A2A cancel one task at a time. None documents cancelling actions already queued or running across all agents. The EU AI Act will require a “stop” button for high-risk systems from 2 December 2027.

            Closest: EG-173, EG-186, EG-237, AH-066 (ServiceNow community page), AH-427

          2. A limit on what all of a firm’s agents can do in total

            • Law in trading (RTS 6, SEC 15c3-5)
            • Announced work: none

            Spend caps meter the vendor’s API cost, not the value agents move. AP2 budgets cover one user’s mandate. Many agents, each within its limits, can add up to an unbounded exposure. This is the only trading law with no partial agent equivalent.

            Closest: CD-852, AH-524

          3. A value cap on tool calls that move money, outside payments

            • Law in trading (RTS 6)
            • Announced work: none outside payments

            AP2 and ACP cap one payment. No stored page documents a cap on a refund, order or transfer made through an ordinary tool call. AWS and Databricks policy engines could express one, but neither documents it (our inference).

            Closest: AH-523, AH-532, BF-027

          4. Two-person approval is rare, and approvers are increasingly models

            • Practice in trading and payments
            • Announced work: none on two people

            145 controls in 26 products require a human. Most action approvals are one confirmation, often by the person who started the run. Copilot Studio offers multistage approvals that combine human and AI reviews, and ServiceNow sub-approvals and Entra multi-stage approval cover AI assets and agent access. Meanwhile Codex can send approval requests to a reviewer agent, a ServiceNow setting lets an AI model review actions, and UiPath reuses stored past human decisions.

            Closest: AH-136, EG-232, EG-181; model reviewers CD-731, EG-250, AH-285. Rests on an inventory search, not on the adjudicated mapping.

          5. Guardrails independent of the agent, and controls only the firm can change

            • Standard (IEC 61511); law in trading (15c3-5)
            • Announced work: partial

            Anthropic documents managed settings as “a client-side control, not a security boundary”, and a local administrator can edit them. 2026 brought server-side checks: Anthropic’s inference hooks (beta) and tool-call evaluation (generally available), AWS and Databricks policy engines. None documents that the check shares no model, host or runtime with the agent.

            Closest: CD-411, CD-682, BF-129, EG-021. Events P-110, P-115, P-085, G-108

          6. A way to check and reverse what an agent did

            • Law in trading (RTS 6)
            • Announced work: none

            Controls that detect risky behaviour and stop the agent exist: Entra blocks risky agents, Agent 365 rules block them, Codex pauses unsafe tasks and ServiceNow deactivates vulnerable tools. What is missing is a check on the business effects of completed actions, and a way back: Gemini Code Assist says there is no undo for changes outside the IDE, and rollback controls roll back agent versions, not their effects.

            Detect and stop: EG-169, EG-171, AH-171, CD-719, EG-239. No reversal: CD-1042; version rollback AH-235, BF-142

          7. Segregation of duties across an agent’s tools

            • Product feature (SAP, Oracle)
            • Announced work: none

            Lakera detects one fixed risky combination: an agent that can read confidential data, take in untrusted content and communicate externally. No vendor offers a rule like “no agent may hold both create-vendor and approve-payment”.

            Closest: EG-062

          8. Limits on delegation across separate agents

            • Design test, no home rule
            • Announced work: protocol sign-in only

            Inside one product, subagents are controlled: Claude Code limits which subagents may run and screens a delegated task before it starts, ServiceNow sets numeric subagent limits, and Cursor can deny a subagent at start. Across separate agents, A2A and MCP only authenticate each hop, and Workday forbids proxy delegation outright. Nothing limits how deep such a chain goes, narrows scope as a task passes along, or ties a downstream action to the original person across more than one hop.

            Subagents: CD-302, CD-447, EG-255, CD-835. Across agents: AH-485, AH-188, AH-190, AH-192

          9. A repeated-error disable that only a person can lift

            • Law in trading (RTS 6)
            • Announced work: none

            Trading systems must be “automatically disabled until re-enabled by a designated staff member” after repeated executions. Some agent counters do persist: Intercom switches Fin off for the rest of the billing period at a hard limit, Claude Code’s auto mode falls back to asking after repeated blocks, and spend caps pause usage until the next month. None disables an agent after repeated errors until a named person turns it back on.

            Persisting counters: AH-405, CD-445, CD-027, CD-212. Per-run caps: AH-279, AH-435, BF-197

          10. Accuracy, data-reach and stored-data controls spread slowly in our timeline

            • Low coverage
            • Announced work: none in the record since 2025

            Answer accuracy is documented by 14 of 46 products, the fewest. It and stored data had no new release event in 2026, and data reach never had more than three vendors in the timeline. Part of this is how we collected: the timeline has no events for ServiceNow, Workday, IBM or Gemini Enterprise, and its collectors read security changelogs.

            Most recent: P-066, AWS Automated Reasoning checks, August 2025

            What these findings rest on
            • Documentation shows what products offer, not what firms switch on or how well a control works.
            • The snapshot is dated October 2026, and vendor documentation changes quickly. Some pages are gated or rendered by script; controls on pages we could not retrieve are missing.
            • 63% of control rows (1,329 of 2,111) were coded by one coder. A spot check of 40 rows found all three codes right on 35; enforcement point is the weakest of the three.
            • Several close agent analogues come from pages that are not product documentation: Credo AI marketing pages, ServiceNow community articles and Salesforce Trailhead modules. None of the ten largest gaps rests only on them.
            • The timeline covers 26 vendors chosen in advance. Dense changelogs add more events, so we count control types, not events. 153 events state no release stage, so counts of general availability are a floor.
            • Mappings to other domains and stage codes are judgements, reported with agreement statistics. Milestone sets in the older regimes are small and were picked by one collector.
            • 95 of 370 product-by-object cells rest on one row, and 28 on a single row coded once.
            • Three products rest wholly on pages that are not reference documentation: Salesforce Agentforce on Trailhead training modules (37 rows), ServiceNow AI Agents on community and store pages (35) and Credo AI on product pages (18). The explorers flag them.
            • The timeline has no events for ServiceNow, Workday, IBM or Gemini Enterprise, so slow growth in autonomy, accuracy and data reach is partly how we collected.
            • Trading is the only comparison domain where we collected binding law, so the law-required gaps are all trading controls by construction.
            Where we departed from the plan
            • The map was built on a balanced sample of 662 controls.
            • 1,329 rows (63%) were coded by one coder, and the 120 held-out rows carry that coder’s code. Clear errors in a spot check: 4 of 24 single-coder rows.
            • Determinism, position and reach were coded for the agent categories and the five enterprise-software domains, not for trading, payments, cloud, email, IT change or industrial safety. The planned determinism-by-reach map of all regimes was not drawn.
            • Timeline events carry object and level codes but no mechanism or enforcement point.
            • No agreement statistic is reported for the reverse list (32 of 83 candidate rows were on both coders’ lists). Two-person approval was never mapped; it rests on an inventory search.
            • In the lineage, the first coder was the collector, not blind.
            • Sample changes: Gemini Code Assist added; Lakera and the archived LLM Guard chosen for the security group; AWS split into three products.
            • Level 4 is defined as “agent-aware” in the timeline and as “identity- or actor-aware” in the lineage.
            • There is no fetch date per row; the snapshot is October 2026.
            • Comparison sources are thin in places: no Visa or Mastercard documentation; industrial safety rests on one HSE bulletin; the NIST change-control text comes from a mirror; IT change from an Atlassian explainer.
            • Three pairs of Internet Archive requests were sent less than five seconds apart, against the pacing rule.
            Checks
            • Two independent card sorts (agreement on objects: adjusted Rand index 0.59), merged by a third model; the merged objects match a data-driven clustering at least as well as either sort (NMI 0.60).
            • Coder agreement on 120 held-out controls plus 24 planted ones: kappa 0.86 for objects, 0.87 for mechanisms, 0.87 for enforcement points; planted-code accuracy 96–100%.
            • Spot check of 40 rows against the stored pages: all 40 quotes verbatim.
            • Comparison mapping: 124 of 154 cells agreed (kappa 0.69), the rest settled by a fresh reviewer. Timeline coding: objects kappa 0.83, sophistication 0.66, availability 0.89. Lineage stages: kappa 0.88.
            • A sensitivity series without archive and press dates moves no turning point by more than a quarter.
