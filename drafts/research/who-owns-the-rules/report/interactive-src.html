Research report · October 2026

Who owns the rules when agents join the business?

AI agents now spend, answer and approve under rules someone has to change. We read 118,526 job postings, 1,167 company filings, 38 practitioner recordings, 19 laws and the documentation of 31 agent products to find out who does.

20of 30 practitioners we recorded describe running agents themselves
15of 26 agent products document an admin role that changes an agent’s rules
0statements or postings describe deciding a rule change across functions

The short version

Agents are run by existing staff and configured in vendors’ tools, while the decision to change their rules has no visible owner

RUNNING

Existing staff run agents, inside their own functions

Running agents is the work people describe most. For every 100 job ads with an AI title, about 86 ads with an ordinary title, such as software engineer or IT administrator, also list agent work. About half of those are technical jobs.

CHANGING

Rules are edited in admin settings, by whoever holds the admin role

In Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Copilot, Agentforce and most others, an admin changes an agent’s limits in the product, under central policy.

DECIDING

The sign-off behind a change has no named holder

No posting or statement describes deciding a rule change across functions. The law will require an overseer of AI from 2027, not an owner of its rules.

Chapter 2 · The tools

Agent rules are changed in admin settings; the decision behind a change is rarely named

    If no one is named, where do the changes happen?

    We read the public documentation of 31 agent products, governance platforms and protocols, as published on 10 October 2026, and coded where an agent’s limits live and who may change them.

    Coding assistants put it plainly: an admin decides what the agent may do

    Seven of eight assistants and coding agents document an owner or admin role that sets agent limits. Organisation settings override the user, and most keep an audit log.

    Business agent platforms mostly do the same

    Seven of twelve document a named permission. Some vendors pair the editor with a decider.

    These guardrails are controlled by your admin and typically signed off by your internal leadership or key decision-makers.Salesforce Agentforce documentation

    Governance platforms record approvals but rarely say who edits

    Inventories, approval workflows and audit trails, but only one of six names the role that changes a limit.

    Protocols leave it to the implementer or the user

    MCP and A2A leave authorisation to whoever builds on them. Payment protocols put the limit in a mandate the user signs.

    Our pre-set test needed two-thirds of products to name an editor; 15 of 26 do, so the evidence is mixed rather than conclusive.

    An agent is a lasting design and many short runs

    On every platform we examined, a design (instructions, tools and limits) persists, while runs created from it last minutes or hours, often many at once. AWS runs each session in its own isolated machine and deletes it at the end. Registries list designs, not runs. So the design and its identity can be owned; each run can only be traced, through logs that link it to its design and to whom it acted for.

    No law asks firms to register agents: the EU registers high-risk AI systems, Colorado regulates automated decision tools, UK bank guidance inventories trading algorithms.

        What these findings rest on
        • Public sources only: postings, filings, recordings, laws and vendor documentation. Rule changes agreed informally inside firms do not appear.
        • 59% of employers in scope have no crawlable job board, including Alphabet, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan.
        • Speech extraction catches about half of what is said, and the statements about changing agents in tools rest mainly on two speakers.
        • Product documentation shows what products allow, not what firms do; four products’ documentation could not be retrieved.
        • No time series: the job-board crawl is one point in time.
