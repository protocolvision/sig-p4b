# Pharmaceutical manufacturing: fact sheet

Scope: automated manufacturing, batch release, change control.
Compiled 2026-10-10. Grades: P = primary source opened, R = reputable secondary opened, S = search summary only. Page fetching was unavailable in this session (DNS failure on every fetch), so every fact below is graded S and should be checked against the cited page before reuse.

## D1 Autonomy and speed

Under "review by exception", validated electronic batch record or MES rules check each step, signature and parameter, and quality assurance reviews only the flagged items; the human reviewer still makes the release decision [1; S]. One consultancy describes a case in which a 150-page batch record was reduced to a three-page exception report [2; S]. Vertex's continuous line for Orkambi, approved by FDA in 2015, used process analytical technology for in-process control and real-time release testing [3; S]. In the EU, Annex 16 (2015 revision, in operation from 15 April 2016) names certification of a batch for release as the primary task of the Qualified Person; tasks may be delegated to quality systems if the QP has "on-going assurance that this reliance is well founded" [4; S]. No source was found giving the speed or cycle time of automated control actions on pharmaceutical lines (not found).

## D2 Consequence of a single automated action

FDA warning letters cite firms under 21 CFR 211.192 for failing to investigate unexplained batch discrepancies, including whether affected batches had already been distributed [5; S]. Under 21 CFR 314.81(b)(1)(ii), a Field Alert Report is due within three working days of learning of a significant change or deterioration in a distributed product [5; S]. In pharmacy compounding devices (not manufacturing lines), a software fault in Baxa's Exacta-Mix 2400 version 1.07 allowed up to an extra 50 ml of an ingredient into an IV solution, described as potentially deadly for newborns, and a 2004 Baxter Logix-CM software anomaly could compound a mixture without one ingredient when two drugs shared an identifier [6; S]. The search found no case of a finished drug recalled because of a manufacturing-line software error, and no example of an automated action moving money or binding a contract (not found).

## D3 How rules are encoded

21 CFR 11.10 requires secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails (11.10(e)), operational system checks that enforce the permitted sequencing of steps (11.10(f)), authority checks that limit who may use the system, sign, alter records or perform an operation (11.10(g)), and device checks on the validity of the source of data input (11.10(h)) [7; S]. Vendor guidance says review by exception works only when execution is "hard-gated" at critical points, and describes lot status as quarantined by default until evidence is verified, with hold, released and rejected as the other states [1; S]. ANSI/ISA-88.01-1995 separates the recipe (materials and processing) from equipment capability, so recipes can be written without knowing how a given machine carries them out [8; S].

## D4 External requirements on behaviour and change

FDA published 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records and signatures) on 20 March 1997, effective 20 August 1997 [9; S]. EU GMP Annex 11 (Computerised Systems, 2011) is in force; the European Commission opened consultation on a revised draft on 7 July 2025 (closing 7 October 2025, per secondary sources) together with a revised Chapter 4 and a new Annex 22 on artificial intelligence, and one commentary says the draft grows from five to nineteen pages with requirements on cybersecurity, alarm management and audit trails [10; S]. FDA issued final guidance "Computer Software Assurance for Production and Quality System Software" in late September 2025 (docket FDA-2022-D-0795), a risk-based approach that supplements the 2002 General Principles of Software Validation and supersedes its Section 6; it covers device production and quality-system software [11; S]. ICH Q12 reached Step 4 in 2019 and was adopted by EMA on 4 March 2020; it defines Established Conditions whose change outside approved ranges triggers a regulatory submission [12; S]. GAMP 5 is ISPE industry guidance, not a regulator's text [13; S].

## D5 Who is involved in changing a rule

Change control in GMP is typically run through a review panel or change control board with quality assurance, regulatory affairs, R&D and manufacturing assessing technical feasibility, regulatory impact and resources [14; S]. Under ICH Q12, a Post-Approval Change Management Protocol is a two-step process: the protocol is agreed with the regulator first, then the change is executed under it and results reported, typically at a lower reporting category [12; S]. A 2017 FDA warning letter cited a firm for running quality investigations as "internal audits" outside the quality unit's oversight, a violation of 21 CFR 211.22(d) [5; S]. A PDA industry paper says EU legal-framework constraints have limited Q12's effect in the EU [12; S].

## D6 Licensed profession

Directive 2001/83/EC Article 48 requires every manufacturing authorisation holder to have at least one Qualified Person, responsible for the duties in Article 51 [15; S]. Article 49 requires a university-level diploma of at least four years (or three and a half years plus one year of training) in pharmacy, medicine, veterinary medicine, chemistry, pharmaceutical chemistry and technology, or biology [15; S]. The exact wording of the practical-experience requirement was not visible in the search results (not found). No source was found on whether a licensed individual must release batches in the United States; the results refer to the firm's quality unit (21 CFR 211.22(d)) [5; S].

## D7 Overrides, exceptions and near misses as data

Failure to investigate under 21 CFR 211.192 is described as the most frequently cited investigation failure in FDA warning letters, with findings that root causes lacked data or were "not formally documented in your deviation system" [5; S]. A GMP industry article says most deviation-related inspection observations reduce to deviation-management failures and lack of CAPA, and links the emphasis on CAPA to ICH Q10 [5; S]. A draft revision of Annex 16 allows a batch with an unplanned deviation to be certified if a risk assessment shows no adverse effect on quality, safety or efficacy [16; S]. The draft Annex 11 adds alarm management and expanded audit-trail obligations [10; S].

## D8 How long in widespread use

The first part of the ISA-88 batch control standard was published in 1995, with Part 2 in 2001 and Part 3 in 2004; adoption began in chemicals and spread to consumer packaged goods and pharmaceuticals [8; S]. A 2008 trade article reports Genentech saving $4 million by reusing batch modules and Eli Lilly cutting standard deviations for cycle time and yield by 50 percent (self-reported) [8; S]. Vertex's 2015 Orkambi approval is described in trade press as the first FDA approval of a drug made on a continuous line [3; S]. The date of first deployment of distributed control systems in pharmaceutical plants was not found.
