# Automotive driver assistance and automated driving: fact sheet

Scope: driver assistance (SAE Level 2), conditional automation (Level 3) and driverless operation (Level 4). Facts are from UNECE, the EU, Germany, the UK and the United States.
Compiled 2026-10-10. Grades: P = primary source opened, R = reputable secondary opened, S = search summary only. Page fetching was unavailable in this session (DNS failure on every fetch), so every fact below is graded S and should be checked against the cited page before reuse.

## D1 Autonomy and speed

Waymo reached 250,000 paid driverless rides a week in April 2025, more than 450,000 by December 2025 and about 500,000 by late March 2026, and completed 14 million trips in 2025 [1; S]. A tracker reports 127 million rider-only autonomous miles through September 2025 and a fleet of more than 3,000 vehicles in December 2025 (secondary figures) [1; S]. Mercedes-Benz's Drive Pilot, a Level 3 system approved under UN R157, was introduced in Germany in May 2022 and certified in Nevada on 26 January 2023 and then California [2; S]. UN R157 (Automated Lane Keeping Systems) originally allowed operation up to 60 km/h; its 01 series amendments, adopted by WP.29 in 2022, raised the maximum to 130 km/h and added lane changes [3; S]. Germany's 2021 Level 4 law requires a technical supervisor who can deactivate the vehicle or enable driving manoeuvres from outside it [4; S].

## D2 Consequence of a single automated action

On 2 October 2023 a Cruise robotaxi in San Francisco ran over a pedestrian who had been thrown into its path by another car, did not detect her, and dragged her more than 20 feet while pulling to the curb [5; S]. In December 2023 Tesla recalled more than 2 million vehicles after a two-year NHTSA investigation of crashes with Autopilot engaged; NPR reported at least 17 deaths [6; S]. Each Waymo ride is a paid trip, so the automated service delivers a commercial transaction without a human driver [1; S]. No example was found of a single automated driving action binding a contract beyond the ride itself (not found).

## D3 How rules are encoded

The R157 01 series added provisions on lane changes, minimal risk manoeuvres, response to emergency vehicles and data collection [3; S]. Tesla's December 2023 over-the-air remedy added driver alerts and limited where Autosteer can be used [6; S]. ISO 26262 classifies hazards into ASIL A to D from severity, exposure and controllability, and ISO 21448 (SOTIF) addresses hazards arising when nothing has failed but the intended function is insufficient [7; S]. Under UN R156, manufacturers identify type-approval-relevant software configurations with an RXSWIN identifier for each vehicle type [8; S]. Under the EU General Safety Regulation, intelligent speed assistance must be switchable off, for example when false warnings arise from bad weather or misleading signs [9; S].

## D4 External requirements on behaviour and change

UN R155 (cybersecurity management system) and R156 (software update management system) apply to new vehicle types from July 2022 and to all new vehicles from July 2024, according to most sources; one manufacturer source gives later EU dates [8; S]. EU Regulation 2019/2144 made intelligent speed assistance and other systems mandatory from 6 July 2022, and from 7 July 2026 pedestrian- and cyclist-detecting emergency braking and advanced driver distraction warning on new vehicles [9; S]. Germany's Autonomous Driving Act entered into force on 28 July 2021 (StVG sections 1d to 1l), with the implementing ordinance AFGBV in 2022 [4; S]. The UK Automated Vehicles Act 2024 received Royal Assent on 20 May 2024 and requires vehicles to pass a "self-driving test" before authorisation [10; S]. ISO 26262 was first published in 2011 and revised in December 2018, and ISO 21448 became a full standard in 2022; these are industry standards, not regulations [7; S].

## D5 Who is involved in changing a rule

Under the UK Act, each authorised vehicle needs an authorised self-driving entity, which one source says is likely to be the manufacturer, operator or software developer [10; S]. R156 requires the manufacturer to implement and operate a software update management system, and R155 a cybersecurity management system [8; S]. Tesla stated it did not agree with NHTSA's analysis but agreed to the recall on 5 December 2023 [6; S]. Cruise's 2024 NHTSA consent order requires a corrective action plan and periodic safety reporting [5; S]. Which internal functions must approve a change to a driving policy was not found.

## D6 Licensed profession

Under the UK Act, a "user-in-charge" is an individual in the vehicle able to operate the controls who is immune from offences arising from the way the vehicle drives while an authorised feature is engaged, but not from non-driving duties such as insurance; vehicles with no user-in-charge must be run by licensed operators [10; S]. Germany requires a technical supervisor for Level 4 vehicles, whose required characteristics are set in the AFGBV; the qualification criteria themselves were not found [4; S]. No source was found in this search on how an ordinary driving licence applies to Level 2 or Level 3 use (not found).

## D7 Overrides, exceptions and near misses as data

NHTSA's Standing General Order 2021-01 requires crash reports for vehicles with ADS or Level 2 ADAS where the system was in use within 30 seconds of a crash meeting severity criteria; the Third Amended Order (issued 24 April 2025, effective 16 June 2025) sets a five-calendar-day reporting window, and NHTSA publishes the data [11; S]. California requires autonomous vehicle testers to file a collision report (form OL 316) within 10 business days and an annual report of disengagements, described as a technology failure that shifts control from the self-driving mode to the safety driver [12; S]. Cruise was fined $1.5 million by NHTSA because its initial crash reports omitted that the vehicle dragged the pedestrian, and separately agreed to a $500,000 fine under a deferred prosecution agreement [5; S]. The R157 01 series added data collection specifications [3; S].

## D8 How long in widespread use

Mitsubishi offered a lidar distance warning on the Debonair in 1992 (one source says 1991), and the 1995 Diamante had the first production adaptive cruise control, which could reduce throttle and downshift but not brake [13; S]. Toyota offered lidar adaptive cruise in Japan in 1997, Mercedes-Benz's Distronic (1999, S-Class) could apply the brakes, and BMW added Active Cruise Control to the 7 Series in 2000; for about a decade the technology stayed on premium flagship models [13; S]. Tesla's 2023 recall covered vehicles produced from 5 October 2012 [6; S]. Level 3 systems reached customers from 2022 (Drive Pilot) and paid Level 4 robotaxi service reached hundreds of thousands of rides a week in 2025 [1; 2; S].
