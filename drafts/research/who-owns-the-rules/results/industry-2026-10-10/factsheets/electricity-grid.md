# Electricity grid: fact sheet

Scope: grid operation, automatic generation control, reliability standards. Facts are from North America (NERC, FERC, PJM, WECC).
Compiled 2026-10-10. Grades: P = primary source opened, R = reputable secondary opened, S = search summary only. Page fetching was unavailable in this session (DNS failure on every fetch), so every fact below is graded S and should be checked against the cited page before reuse.

## D1 Autonomy and speed

PJM training material says its legacy RegA regulation signal is sent every 2 seconds, and a 2011 PJM manual revision lists RegA and RegD on a 2-second scan rate [1; S]. A PJM filing to FERC says committed resources follow dispatch signals that PJM can send every 2 to 10 seconds [1; S]. In the 2003 Northeast blackout, lines and generating units were tripped automatically by protective relays, and the joint task force described the spread as the result of automatic equipment operations, primarily relays and circuit breakers; key lines tripped between 16:05:57 and 16:10:38 on impedance relays responding to overloads rather than faults [2; S]. RTO real-time markets dispatch on five-minute intervals, which FERC Order 825 required them to align with settlement [3; S].

## D2 Consequence of a single automated action

FERC Order 825 (16 June 2016) requires each RTO/ISO to settle real-time energy at the interval at which it dispatches, and to trigger shortage pricing for any interval in which a shortage is indicated; PJM implemented five-minute settlement on 1 April 2018 [3; S]. The 2003 blackout affected about 50 million people, shut down more than 200 power plants and lost about 61,800 MW of customer load; power was not restored for four days in parts of the United States, and parts of Ontario had rolling blackouts for more than a week [2; S]. Automatic underfrequency load shedding programmes, governed by NERC standard PRC-006, disconnect customer load when frequency falls to set points [4; S].

## D3 How rules are encoded

Area Control Error combines net interchange deviation with a frequency-bias term (ACE = ΔNI + bΔf), so each balancing authority can compute it from local measurements and regulate it to zero [5; S]. PRC-006-0 required documented frequency set points as part of underfrequency load-shedding design; PRC-006-5 is the version cited as current, with a WECC regional variance [4; S]. CIP-010 Requirement R1 requires a configuration baseline (operating system or firmware, installed software and versions, custom software, network-accessible ports, security patches) for high- and medium-impact BES Cyber Systems, and requires deviations from it to be authorised and documented [6; S]. An interconnection specification lists PRC-012-2 and requires documentation of remedial action scheme settings for protection and control devices [4; S].

## D4 External requirements on behaviour and change

The Energy Policy Act of 2005 added section 215 to the Federal Power Act, under which FERC certified NERC as the Electric Reliability Organization [7; S]. FERC Order No. 693, issued 16 March 2007 and effective 18 June 2007, approved 83 of 107 proposed reliability standards as mandatory and enforceable and required improvements to 56 of them [7; S]. NERC standards cited above set requirements on automated and protective equipment: PRC-004 (protection system misoperations), PRC-006 (underfrequency load shedding), PRC-012 (remedial action schemes) and CIP-010 (configuration change management) [4; 6; 8; S]. A vendor source states that as of July 2026 CIP-010-4 is the enforceable version [6; S].

## D5 Who is involved in changing a rule

NERC reliability standards are approved by a registered ballot body made up of ten stakeholder segments; approval requires a quorum of 75 percent of the ballot pool and a two-thirds weighted segment majority, according to 2007 and 2008 NERC reports [9; S]. A drafting team reviews comments after each initial ballot, and a recirculation ballot is required when an initial ballot reaches quorum with negative votes that carry comments [9; S]. A 2026 MRO presentation describes proposed changes to the process, including straw polls instead of multiple ballots and revised ballot body voting rules [9; S]. Within a utility, NERC's CIP-010 audit worksheet accepts a change request record with electronic authorisation by the person holding approval authority as evidence [6; S].

## D6 Licensed profession

NERC standard PER-003 requires real-time operating positions of Reliability Coordinators, Balancing Authorities and Transmission Operators that control the Bulk Electric System to be staffed by holders of a valid NERC System Operator Certification [10; S]. Non-certified staff at such positions must work under direct supervision of a certified operator who keeps ultimate responsibility; PER-003-0 (2005) set the original requirement [10; S]. This is an industry certification required by a mandatory standard; no government licence for system operators was found.

## D7 Overrides, exceptions and near misses as data

Under PRC-004, entities must review all protection system operations to identify, report and correct misoperations, including failure to operate on time, operation for faults outside the zone, and unintentional operation without a fault [8; S]. Misoperations are reported to NERC's MIDAS database, which replaced webCDMS on 1 July 2016, through quarterly submittals under a Section 1600 data request, and NERC uses the data to build performance metrics [8; S]. NERC's Event Analysis Program (version 4.0, effective 2020) assigns events to categories, and lessons learned have been published since 2010; WECC logs and closes Category 0 events, writes a brief report for Categories 1 and 2, and an event analysis report for Categories 3 to 5 [11; S].

## D8 How long in widespread use

A NERC white paper (2015) says tie-line bias control had been the preferred control method in North America for 75 years and that the term Area Control Error was developed in the early 1950s [5; S]. University of Toronto material dates area-by-area decentralised control to the 1940s, and the distributed biased net-interchange concept is credited to N. Cohn and R. Brandt [5; S]. Mandatory, enforceable reliability standards date from Order 693 in 2007 [7; S].
