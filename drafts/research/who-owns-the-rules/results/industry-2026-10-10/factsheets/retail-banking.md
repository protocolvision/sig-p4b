# Retail banking: fact sheet

Scope: automated credit decisions, payments, anti-money-laundering monitoring. Facts are mainly from the United States, with EU rules on automated credit scoring.
Compiled 2026-10-10. Grades: P = primary source opened, R = reputable secondary opened, S = search summary only. Page fetching was unavailable in this session (DNS failure on every fetch), so every fact below is graded S and should be checked against the cited page before reuse.

## D1 Autonomy and speed

A vendor source says card authorisations must complete in under 300 milliseconds end to end across networks (vendor claim) [1; S]. Visa's fiscal 2016 Form 10-K says VisaNet is capable of handling more than 65,000 transactions per second [2; S]. The Federal Reserve's FedNow instant payment service went live on 20 July 2023, available at any hour on any day, with 35 early-adopting banks and credit unions; The Clearing House's RTP network launched in 2017 [3; S]. Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter and Freddie Mac's Loan Prospector are automated underwriting systems for mortgages; Fannie Mae dropped its 620 minimum credit score requirement for Desktop Underwriter loans effective 16 November 2025 [4; S]. FinCEN received about 4.6 million suspicious activity reports in fiscal 2023, an average of 12,600 a day, of which about 2.5 million came from depository institutions [5; S].

## D2 Consequence of a single automated action

In April 2024 Citigroup credited a customer account with $81 trillion instead of $280; it was reversed within hours and no money left the bank, according to the Financial Times (reported 28 February 2025) [6; S]. Under ECOA and Regulation B, adverse actions taken with complex algorithms, including denying an application, terminating an account, changing its terms or refusing a credit-limit increase, require a statement of specific reasons (CFPB Circular 2022-03, 26 May 2022) [7; S]. The Court of Justice of the EU held on 7 December 2023 (C-634/21, SCHUFA) that a credit agency's automated score is itself an automated individual decision under GDPR Article 22(1) where a third party's decision to enter into, perform or end a contract depends decisively on it [8; S].

## D3 How rules are encoded

New York's Part 504 requires transaction monitoring with "detection scenarios with threshold values and amounts", documentation of the assumptions, parameters and thresholds behind each scenario, and end-to-end pre- and post-implementation testing covering governance, data mapping, transaction coding, detection scenario logic, model validation, data input and programme output [9; S]. In the Citigroup case, a payment held over sanctions concerns needed manual processing on a backup screen whose amount field was pre-filled with 15 zeros; Citi said its detective controls caught the error and its preventive controls would have stopped funds from leaving [6; S]. Early automated mortgage underwriting systems in the late 1980s and early 1990s used each lender's internal credit scorecard, and later systems combined FICO scores with additional rules [4; S].

## D4 External requirements on behaviour and change

The Federal Reserve (SR 11-7) and the OCC (Bulletin 2011-12) issued supervisory guidance on model risk management on 4 April 2011, and the FDIC adopted it in 2017; secondary sources report it was superseded in April 2026 by SR 26-2 and OCC Bulletin 2026-13, which was not verified [10; S]. NYDFS issued Part 504 on 30 June 2016, effective 1 January 2017, with the first annual board resolution or senior-officer compliance finding due 15 April 2018 [11; S]. The EU AI Act lists AI used to evaluate creditworthiness or establish credit scores as high-risk (Annex III, point 5(b)), triggering risk management, data governance, logging, transparency, human oversight (Article 14) and deployer obligations; sources conflict on whether these apply from 2 August 2026 or were deferred to December 2027 by the AI Omnibus [12; S]. CFPB Circular 2022-03 states that a creditor cannot justify noncompliance on the ground that its technology is too complicated or opaque, and that post-hoc explanations must be validated [7; S].

## D5 Who is involved in changing a rule

SR 11-7 is summarised as resting on sound model development, effective challenge through independent validation before a model enters production and periodically after, and governance with board accountability [10; S]. Under Part 504 either the board of directors or a senior officer certifies the transaction monitoring and filtering programme; the proposed rule had allowed only senior compliance officers to certify, and commentators say the rule exposes certifying individuals to enforcement [11; S]. In the Citigroup case, a payments employee and a second checker both missed the error, a third employee caught it about 90 minutes after posting, and the bank reported it as a near miss to the Federal Reserve and the OCC [6; S]. Specific functions required to approve a change to a credit or monitoring rule were not found.

## D6 Licensed profession

The SAFE Act requires individual mortgage loan originators to be registered with NMLS (if employed by a federally insured depository institution) or state-licensed, with fingerprints, an FBI background check and 20 hours of pre-licensing education; employed underwriters and processors working under a licensed originator's supervision are exempt, while independent underwriters must be licensed [13; S]. No licence was found for the people who own automated credit decisions, payment release or AML alert disposition (not found).

## D7 Overrides, exceptions and near misses as data

An internal Citigroup report cited by the Financial Times counted 10 near misses of $1 billion or more in the prior year, down from 13 (sources disagree on whether the year was 2023 or 2024) [6; S]. Part 504 requires supporting schedules and data for the annual certification to be kept for five years [11; S]. FinCEN publishes annual counts of suspicious activity reports (4.6 million in fiscal 2023) [5; S]. No source was found on how overrides of automated credit decisions are recorded (not found).

## D8 How long in widespread use

The first automated mortgage underwriting systems appeared in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and by the mid-to-late 1990s Freddie Mac and then Fannie Mae were building systems on FICO scores [4; S]. VisaNet's stated capacity of more than 65,000 transactions per second dates from 2016 [2; S]. Instant account-to-account payment networks in the US date from 2017 (RTP) and 2023 (FedNow) [3; S]. NYDFS transaction-monitoring rules date from 2016 [11; S]. The first deployment of automated AML transaction monitoring was not found.
