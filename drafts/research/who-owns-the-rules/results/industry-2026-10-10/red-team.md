## 1. Is algorithmic trading an artefact of the weights?

Mostly no. Trading comes first with equal weights, with any one dimension dropped, and in 80% of 20,000 random weightings. D6 is not doing too much work: it supplies 1 point of the 2.5-point margin, and without it trading still leads, 57.5 to 56.0. Banking overtakes only at a D6 weight of 9 or more; the grid only at a D7 weight of 6 or more.

The weak point is the scores in a few cells, not the weights:

- Scorer B tied trading and the grid at 59. The gap comes from D2, D5 and D6, less D7.
- Both scorers gave trading D5 = 3 while writing "change committee not found". E-commerce and automotive got 1 for the same "not found". Two defensible changes put the grid first, 58.0 to 57.5: trading D5 to 2, and the grid's D2 to 4 from scorer A (B already gave 4, since relay trips cut off 50 million people).
- No dimension measures what separates trading from agent-run business: varied actions, many firms, firms already registered, a central venue.

Trading or the grid comes first in 88% of weightings; trading over the grid is close to a coin toss, and several verdicts depend on it (§2).

## 3. Does the translation to agents overreach?

- **Uniform, money-measured actions.** Limits, kill switches and "material change" work because every action is an order with a price and size. Agent actions (messages, code, data access) have no common unit; only spending does.
- **A few registered firms.** 15c3-5, Series 57 and UK certification reached registered broker-dealers through existing exam machinery. Outside finance no register or exam body exists to carry "certify the agent's designer".
- **Exchanges as chokepoints.** The first structures sat at venues (circuit breakers 1988, CME kill switch, LULD). Agents have no exchange; the nearest are payment networks and model providers, yet the translation puts the gateway with the firm, "not the vendor's".
- **Harm to others.** Regulators acted because errors hit other participants (Flash Crash; Citi's $1.4bn of European sales). Most agent errors fall on the firm or its customers; a private "Knight Capital moment" may produce no rule.
- **The analogue misses its own window.** Knight (August 2012) to Series 57 approval (April 2016) is 3.7 years, 4.5 to effect: outside "within 3 years".
- **The incident clock.** FIA's controls (April 2010, lever "none") preceded the Flash Crash, as did the 15c3-5 proposal (January 2010; check). The 1987 crash produced no in-firm role for 17 years.
- **"Never belonged to a practitioner"** is contradicted by the counter-examples and by Citi's 2026 global head (coded PE;RD, practitioner role).

## 6. The one piece of evidence that would most change the forecast

Whether agent-run business has an exchange-like chokepoint: binding rules from a payment network, model or cloud provider requiring every customer to limit, log and be able to stop agent actions. If yes, trading's sequence (venue controls and industry standards, then in-firm controls and named owners) carries beyond finance and the incident clock matters less. If no, the forecast shrinks to regulated finance, and the control is the better guide for everyone else: duties not roles, no internal decider, structures only where an outsider imposes them.
