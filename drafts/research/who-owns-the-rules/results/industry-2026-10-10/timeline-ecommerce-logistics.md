# E-commerce and retail logistics: who owns the rules (1990s to Oct 2026)

25 dated events in `timeline-ecommerce-logistics.csv`, all from web search results. Layers: PV = noticing deviations, PE = designing and changing rules, RD = deciding changes. Grades: P = primary (regulator, statute or company text), R = reputable secondary (news, law-firm summary), S = single weak source (none kept).

## PV: noticing deviations

- **Fraud and payments:** card-network monitoring. Visa's VAMP (2025) is the latest step in a line that runs from PCI SSC (2006). It is external and backed by a gatekeeper rule. Inside merchants, fraud work stays at practitioner level (mid), with the Merchant Risk Council (2002) as the industry network.
- **Marketplace sellers:** mandated systems backed by law. These are P2B complaint handling (2020), INFORM seller verification (2023) and DSA risk assessments with external audit (2023; Temu fined 2026). Amazon's Project Zero (2019) and Counterfeit Crimes Unit (2020, senior) had no stated mandate.
- **Warehouse quotas and robotics:** mostly external. Regulators did the noticing: California's Labor Commissioner (2022), CNIL (2023) and OSHA's company-wide ergonomic assessments (2024). Per a 2024 Senate report, Amazon's Project Soteria linked speed to injuries but changed no policy.
- **Pricing engines:** none inside firms. Deviations were noticed by customers (2000), researchers (2011), prosecutors (2015) and consumer groups (2025).

## PE: designing and changing rules

- **Fraud:** the card brands' standards body (PCI SSC, 2006; gatekeeper rule).
- **Warehouses:** law now names a role. New York's qualified ergonomist and injury reduction programme (2024, mid) cover pace. ANSI/RIA R15.08 (2020, industry body) covers robots. Amazon's Time Off Task change (2021) was an executive decision with no standing body.
- **Seller pricing rules:** increasingly set from outside. The Bundeskartellamt (2026) limits Amazon's price controls to exceptional cases, and China (2022) requires algorithm review systems.

## RD: deciding changes

- **Marketplaces:** the DSA Art. 41 head of compliance (2023, law). It is the one named senior role, reporting to the management body. Monitoring trustees under the EU (2022) and UK commitments are external bodies.
- **Pricing:** no designated decider. Decisions are ad hoc, made after a backlash (Amazon 2000, Instacart 2025). Per the FTC (2023), Project Nessie was switched on and off internally with no disclosed governance. That trial is due to start 13 Oct 2026.
- **Warehouses:** no internal decision body found. Decisions are pushed by law and enforcement.
