# Algorithmic trading: who owns the rules (US, UK, EU), 1971 to October 2026

Companion to `timeline-algorithmic-trading.csv` (55 events). References give the year and a short label from the CSV. All events come from search results; no pages were opened. Grades: P primary excerpt, R reputable secondary, S search summary only.

**Spread of the automation.** Electronic quotation began in 1971 and electronic order routing in 1976. Algorithms became the majority of trading by 2009, when HFT was estimated at about 70% of US equity volume (2009, Chicago Fed). Lags below run from 2009; from 1976, add about 33 years.

## PV: noticing deviations

- **Main structure today:** real-time monitoring and a kill function at each firm (2018, RTS 6 Art. 2/12/16), plus surveillance with trained analysts and suspicious-order reports (2016, MAR Art. 16).
  - In banks this sits in first-line "E-Trading Risk & Control" teams in the Markets COO function (2021, Citi role; S). When the title first appeared is unknown.
- **Seniority:** mid; team heads senior.
- **Lever:** law, reinforced by incidents and fines (2012 Knight; 2024 Citi, where alerts escalated too slowly).
- **Earlier forms:**
  - Voluntary program-trade identification at 6 of 10 firms (1988, GAO).
  - NYSE program trading reports, ended 2009.
  - A registered operator ID per automated system (2009, CME Tag 50).
- **Lag:** 7 years (2016) to 9 years (2018).

## PE: designing and changing limits and checks

- **Main structure today:** pre-trade controls under the broker's direct and exclusive control, reviewed annually (2010, Rule 15c3-5). Algorithm designers are registered or certified:
  - US: Securities Trader registration (Series 57) under a supervising principal (2016, FINRA 16-21).
  - UK: algorithmic trading certification function (2016, FCA SM&CR; extended 2019).
  - EU/UK: controlled deployment, an algorithm inventory and a definition of "material change" (2018, RTS 6; 2018, FCA report).
- **Seniority:** mid.
- **Lever:** law and SRO rules, after incidents (2010 Flash Crash; 2012 Knight). Before that, voluntary standards only (2010, FIA).
- **Lag:** 1–9 years (2010–2018).
- **Retreating:** UK certification may be cut (2025, CP25/21); the US dropped Reg SCI for broker-dealers (2025) and Reg AT (2015 row). AI goes into existing reviews, not new roles (2026, ESMA; 2026, SR 26-2).

## RD: deciding rule changes across functions

- **Main structure today:**
  - UK: the board or its risk committee approves algo governance, and a named Senior Manager (SMF) is accountable (2018, PRA SS5/18).
  - EU/UK: a governance arrangement approves deployments and updates, and trading is kept separate from risk and compliance (2018, RTS 6).
  - Each function signs off its own risks (2018, SS5/18 approval).
  - US: the CEO certifies the controls annually (2010, 15c3-5), building on NASD 3013 (2004).
- **Seniority:** executive (US CEO); board and SMF (UK).
- **Lever:** UK/EU law and guidance; US law plus personal liability (2016, Wilson-Davis; 2016, Merrill).
- **Lag:** 1 year (US, 2010); 9 years (UK, 2018).
- **Gaps:**
  - Minimal board involvement (2021, Central Bank of Ireland).
  - Thin board information, and inventories omitting who may operate each algorithm (2025, FCA review).
  - Supervisors now request validation reports directly (2026, AFM).
  - The second line is moving up: a global head of e-trading risk now sets the framework (2026, Citi; S).

## Notes

- **Insurance:** no algorithm-specific insurance found; only Lloyd's rogue-trader cover for human traders (2008).
- **Customer pressure:**
  - Buy-side questionnaires on broker algorithms (2016, AFME/IA).
  - Shareable algorithm certificates (2026, FIX; S).
