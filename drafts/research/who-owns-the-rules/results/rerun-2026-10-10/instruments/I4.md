# I4: what rule texts require, who they name, and who owns changes

## Method
- 111 provisions were read and coded from 19 rule texts (`I4-required-functions.csv`, revision 2; coding rules in `I4-coverage.md`): rule type, binding or not, addressee, whether the text names a specific person, function or body to hold the duty (`names_role`), whether it governs how the system's rules, limits or parameters are changed (`regulates_changes`), and who the text makes responsible for deciding those changes (`change_owner`).
- Every quote in the source file was checked by script against the source text and is 40 words or fewer. Provisions are grouped here into five families by the rule they come from (trading and markets; grid operations; AI law and standards; lab self-policies; one platform rule); the family labels are mine, the codes are the file's.
- Counts are provisions, not rules. Long-format data: `I4-tables.csv`.

## Results

### Totals (111 provisions)
- Rule type: law, regulation or supervisory rule 66; supervisory guidance 18; voluntary standard or framework 13; lab self-policy 13; platform or commercial rule 1. Binding: yes 67, no 44.
- Addressee: investment firm 53; other 13; lab itself 13; provider/developer 10 (plus 6 coded to both provider and deployer); deployer/user firm 8; grid entity 7; platform seller 1.
- Names a role, function or body: yes 54, no 57. Regulates changes: yes 32, no 78, unclear 1 (FCA SYSC 27.7, definition not in the file).
- Of the 32 that regulate changes, 15 name a change owner and 17 name none ("the firm" only).

### Who the texts name
- Trading and markets (31 of 53 provisions name someone): risk control, risk management and compliance functions (RTS 6 Arts 1(c), 3(4), 9(2)-(3), 13(6), 16(2)-(3), 17); a person designated by senior management (RTS 6 Arts 5(2), 11); traders responsible for the algorithm (RTS 6 Art 17); an algorithm owner with sign-off by Front Office, Risk Management and other control functions (PRA SS5/18 3.2-3.3); a Senior Management Function holder (PRA SS5/18 2.5); the Chief Executive Officer for an annual certification (SEC 15c3-5 (e)(2)); a registered Securities Trader (FINRA 16-21).
- Grid operations (7 of 7 name someone): System Operators, Operations Support Personnel, Generator Operator dispatch personnel, NERC-certified System Operator (PER-005-2 R1-R6; PER-003-2 R1-R3). No grid provision was coded as regulating changes to the system's rules.
- AI law and standards (5 of 37 name someone): natural persons assigned human oversight (EU AI Act Arts 14(1), 14(5), 26(2)); an individual designated by the deployer (Colorado 6-1-1701(15)); executive leadership (NIST AI RMF GOVERN 2.3). The EU AI Act provisions on documentation, risk management, logging, monitoring and incident reporting fall on "the provider" or "the deployer" without naming a function.
- Lab self-policies (11 of 13 name someone): Responsible Scaling Officer, CEO, Board, Long-Term Benefit Trust, third-party reviewer (Anthropic); Safety Advisory Group, OpenAI Leadership, Board Safety and Security Committee (OpenAI); "appropriate corporate governance bodies" (DeepMind).

### Dated quotes
- RTS 6 Art 5(2), applies from 3 Jan 2018 (C217f8b25): "A person designated by the senior management of the investment firm shall authorise the deployment or substantial update of an algorithmic trading system, trading algorithm or algorithmic trading strategy."
- PRA SS5/18 3.2-3.3, effective 30 Jun 2018 (Cef3dde3f): "each algorithm to have assigned owners, who are accountable for the algorithm's use and performance".
- SEC 15c3-5 (e)(2), 15 Nov 2010 (C9e5e0522): "The Chief Executive Officer (or equivalent officer) of the broker or dealer shall, on an annual basis, certify that such risk management controls and supervisory procedures comply with paragraphs (b) and (c) of this section".
- NERC PER-003-2 R1-R3, FERC approved 21 Nov 2018 (C84c34c54): "Each Reliability Coordinator shall staff its Real-time operating positions performing Reliability Coordinator reliability-related tasks with System Operators who have demonstrated minimum competency".
- EU AI Act Art 26(2), Chapter III from 2 Aug 2026 as enacted (C9b1da45a): "Deployers shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support."
- Colorado SB26-189, takes effect 1 Jan 2027 (CO-SB26-189-act): "MEANINGFUL HUMAN REVIEW MEANS REVIEW BY A INDIVIDUAL DESIGNATED BY THE DEPLOYER WHO HAS AUTHORITY TO APPROVE, MODIFY, OR OVERRIDE A CONSEQUENTIAL DECISION".
- Anthropic RSP v3.4, effective 8 Jul 2026 (Cf9d2715e): "Changes to the RSP will be proposed by the CEO and RSO, and approved by the Board in consultation with the LTBT."
- OpenAI Preparedness Framework v2, 15 Apr 2025 (C3c1aad5c): "The SAG reviews proposed changes to the Preparedness Framework and makes a recommendation that is processed according to the standard decision-making process."
- DeepMind FSF v3.1, 17 Apr 2026 (Ce9065d39): "The updated version and framework assessment will be reviewed by the appropriate corporate governance bodies."

## Data gaps
- Not read or not coded: RTS 6 annexes; EU AI Act annexes and recitals (other articles keyword-searched only); Colorado exemptions in 6-1-1707; California SB 53 (not acquired, site blocks scripts); ISO/IEC 42001 (purchase page only); Mastercard agentic commerce guide (needs a browser, no text); the DeepMind web page (unreadable); the Amazon Agent Policy text itself (1 row coded from a forum notice).
- NERC PER-003-1 and PER-003-0 failed to download (HTTP 404). NERC PER-005-2 effective date is computed (about 1 Jul 2016), not stated in the text. The Omnibus publication date and the FCA multi-firm review date are not in the files; the 2025 FCA review date is absent.
- Only one version of the OpenAI framework (v2) is in the corpus. The SEC, FINRA, PRA, RTS 6 and NERC texts address trading firms or grid entities; only the EU AI Act and Colorado bind a firm for deploying AI.
- A firm-level duty ("the investment firm shall set limits") is coded `change_owner` = none named; counting those as owners would raise the owner count by 17 rows. The role-naming rule counts untitled designations ("a person designated by senior management") as naming a role.
- The coding is by a single reader with a second-model review noted in `review/I4-review.md`; inter-coder agreement was not measured.
