# Executive summary: who owns the rules when agents join the business

Protocols for Business · rerun of 10 October 2026 · ten points for a chief executive. Confidence in brackets. Sources: `findings.md` (F1–F7), `recommendation.md`.

1. **The problem.** AI agents now act under rules: what they may spend, see and say, and when a person steps in. When a rule gets in the way, someone has to decide the change. We asked who does that work today, and whether AI is reshaping organisations the way DevOps did.

2. **The approach.** We crawled 118,526 job postings from 218 employers (37% of the Fortune 500 and 72% of a venture-backed software sample). We also read 1,167 annual filings, 38 practitioner recordings, 19 laws and policies, and the documentation of 12 vendors. Models extracted about 305,000 duties. Two coders from different model families, tested on hidden seeded cases, coded them blind. All rules were fixed in advance, and a red team argued against every result. The public record cannot show rule changes agreed informally inside firms, so the next step is field work.

3. **Running agents is common. Changing their rules is almost invisible.** In 771 timestamped practitioner statements, 150 describe running agents, 21 setting what agents may do, 1 finding unwritten rules and none deciding a rule change across functions. Postings show the same pattern. (High for the near-absence; medium for the shares.)

4. **Existing staff and new AI titles share the work about evenly.** Existing-title postings with a confirmed agent duty run at 0.86 (0.63–1.15) of new AI-titled postings. Most of them are technical jobs. 81% of employers have an AI title, but these are only about 2.5% of postings. (Low to medium.)

5. **The one place rule design forms a stable bundle of work is AI security review.** Security architects review AI systems before and after deployment, mostly in regulated finance. That bundle rests heavily on one posting. No stable bundle joins rule work with running agents across functions. (Low.)

6. **The law will ask for an overseer, not an owner of the rules.** The EU AI Act (from December 2027) requires deployers of high-risk AI to assign trained people who can oversee it, and Colorado (from January 2027) requires on-request review by a designated person who can override a consequential decision. Neither names anyone to decide rule changes. Only trading rules and AI labs' own policies do that. (High on what the texts say.)

7. **Vendors set the pace.** Agent certifications and vendor "owner" and "sponsor" roles (2025) came before binding law (2027). Mentions of agents in 10-Ks went from almost none to 6.75% of filers in 2026, and vendors supply about 60% of the agent passages in 10-Ks, mostly describing products. (Low to medium for the order; high for the counts.)

8. **Whether this is DevOps again is still open.** The pre-registered pattern test is unreliable, because 10 of 15 features could not be scored. Even a clean run would confirm DevOps only about 5% of the time if it were true. Speakers say existing staff took on the work to coordinate functions, which fits DevOps. Postings ask for scarce AI-specific skill and name AI teams, which fits a scarcity boom. Field work has to settle this tension. (Inconclusive. This is not evidence against the hypothesis.)

9. **Forecast.** At least 15% of 10-K filers will use agent language in 2027 (0.60). Colorado's overseer duty will take effect as signed (0.70). The 2028 US occupation list will have no AI-agent or AI-governance occupation, so the work stays a duty and does not become a job (0.70). At least one agent-scoped vendor certification will be retired or renamed by 2029 (0.75).

10. **Recommendation.** Don't hire a senior "protocol lead"; keep agent running with the staff in each function (medium). Name an owner for agent limits, starting in the security and governance review, the one place the work has been observed so far; that observation rests largely on one posting (low). Keep a register of agents and their limits across vendors and under the firm's control (low to medium; untested against simply using vendor registries). Prepare to name trained overseers before the 2027 deadlines (high on what the law requires). Run a small exception-review pilot that logs what changes and how long it takes: finding and deciding rule changes are design bets, and that log is their test (low).
