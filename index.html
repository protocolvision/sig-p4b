Protocols for Business

The Special Interest Group in Protocols for Business studies how businesses use protocols, how they could use them better, and what we learn by seeing a firm as a set of protocols. Since mid-2025 our case has been AI adoption, and in particular the data operations protocols that decide whether it holds up over time.

The group began in 2025 as one of the first three special interest groups at Summer of Protocols, alongside memory and formal theory. Our notes and essays appear in its magazine, Protocolized. The group is now a research group of the Protocol Institute, which charters groups on protocol themes and funds time-bound projects. The group has two projects in PI's 2027 prospectus: AI Native Data Operations (Rafael Fernandez), described below, and Cognitive Ergonomics (Timber Stinson-Schroff).

2027 Research Focus: AI Native Data Operations

AI adoption now has hundreds of reports, adoption models, and case studies. That creates a second problem: working out which ideas and evidence fit a specific organization and rest on a consistent operating philosophy. Our approach treats data operations protocols as the place where effort pays off most. We have built a capability maturity model backed by case studies, published as the Durable AI Adoption guide. We have tested it in two pilot engagements, one with a water utility and one in construction.

In 2027 we aim to systematize the models and playbooks, field-test them in several more domains, and publish validated methods and case studies as open source. We are looking for research sponsors, and for partners in specific domains who want to apply these methods through consulting engagements.

Research Questions

  • What is an AI-native, post-platform organization, and how does it differ from the scale, process-redesign, and platform paradigms that came before?
  • Where does data operations work give the most leverage in AI adoption?
  • When does an agent become a member of an organization, and how is its discretion delegated, checked, and governed?
  • What happens to the AI industry's information networks when reading is free and value moves to routing, provenance, and signatures?
  • What new forms of collaboration, such as stigmergy, signal broadcasting, and swarms, emerge once people and agents share a data environment?

The group works on two tracks. The reading group runs for a year, October 2026 to October 2027, and builds toward a thesis of the AI-native, post-platform organization. Each session pairs a classic text with a response from our own writing. The case studies are the practical work: two pilot engagements, one on California water rate data and one on construction bids from PDF solicitations, which we are writing up as open case studies.

We meet every other Monday. Readings are circulated in advance. Sessions start with 15–20 minutes of silent reading and then 30–35 minutes of discussion.
