Protocols for Business

The Special Interest Group in Protocols for Business is a research group of the Protocol Institute. We study how businesses actually coordinate, through the protocols beneath the org chart, and what AI is doing to those protocols.

Or join us on Discord in #protocols-for-business.

Protocol vision

Our method is to look at an organization through its protocols. An org chart shows who has authority, a strategy shows intent, and a software stack shows tools. Protocols show what actually recurs: how work moves, how decisions get made, how data is found, checked, and trusted. Recurrence is the clue. When the same thing keeps going wrong, or keeps going surprisingly right, in the same place, there is a protocol underneath it.

Good protocols are hard to see. They fade into the background and produce things that don't happen: the error caught, the dispute that never started. So protocol vision is a trained kind of attention, and the group trains it on three tracks:

  • Readings sharpen it. A year-long syllabus that builds toward a thesis of the AI-native, post-platform organization. Each classic text is paired with a recent discussion from Protocol Institute members.
  • Observations train it. These are the reps. Every session, each participant brings one business protocol they observed. Over the year that makes an inventory of about a hundred.
  • Case studies apply it. These are the heavy lifts. Every participant carries one case study through the year, written to a shared template. Water rate data and construction bids are the worked examples.

Our current focus is AI adoption. AI tools unsettle the protocols a business runs on, and that briefly makes them visible again. It gives us a rare chance to see how a firm really works, and to help redesign it.

Where we come from

The group began in 2025 as one of the first three special interest groups at Summer of Protocols, alongside memory and formal theory. Our notes and essays appear in its magazine, Protocolized. The group is now a research group of the Protocol Institute, which charters groups on protocol themes and funds time-bound projects. The group has two projects in PI's 2027 prospectus: AI Native Data Operations (Rafael Fernández), described below, and Cognitive Ergonomics (Timber Stinson-Schroff).

2027 Research Focus: AI Native Data Operations

AI adoption now has hundreds of reports, adoption models, and case studies. That creates a second problem: working out which ideas and evidence fit a specific organization and rest on a consistent operating philosophy. Our approach treats data operations protocols as the place where effort pays off most. We have built a capability maturity model backed by case studies, published as the Durable AI Adoption guide. We have tested it in two pilot engagements, one with a water utility and one in construction.

In 2027 we aim to systematize the models and playbooks, field-test them in several more domains, and publish validated methods and case studies as open source. We are looking for research sponsors, and for partners in specific domains who want to apply these methods through consulting engagements.

Research Questions

  • What is an AI-native, post-platform organization, and how does it differ from the scale, process-redesign, and platform paradigms that came before?
  • Where does data operations work give the most leverage in AI adoption?
  • When does an agent become a member of an organization, and how is its discretion delegated, checked, and governed?
  • What happens to the AI industry's information networks when reading is free and value moves to routing, provenance, and signatures?
  • What new forms of collaboration, such as stigmergy, signal broadcasting, and swarms, emerge once people and agents share a data environment?

How sessions run

We meet every other Monday for about an hour, sometimes longer. Readings go out in advance with the session email.

  • 15–20 minSilent reading. Everyone reads the core text, or the circulated excerpt, together.
  • 20 minRound-robin. Each person gives one reaction to the reading and presents the business protocol they observed since last time.
  • 20–25 minFeature. One of: a show-and-tell of AI-native tooling someone is using, a member's case study progress, or a guest presentation from another Protocol Institute research group.
