Initial reading plan

The question for the year: how do organizations, teams, and people coordinate through what they emit, and what changes when agents read and write those signals too?

It starts with what agents are in practice and how nature coordinates without a manager. It then looks at what systems emit, how to see hardness in real incidents, and how to design the hard core. It ends with how industries keep that core alive, set against business process re-engineering.

  1. I What agents are, in practice

    Recent incidents and specifications show what agents can see and do, and what changes for the people working beside them.

    4 sessions, show sample readings
    • The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead OpenAI, 2026
    • Patterns and problems in multiagent systems Anthropic, 2026

  2. II Natural coordination

    Cells coordinate through thresholds, feedback and a small conserved core with wide variety at the edges.

    3 sessions, show sample readings
    • Quorum Sensing in Bacteria Miller & Bassler, 2001
    • Reverse Engineering of Biological Complexity Csete & Doyle, Science, 2002

  3. III Emissions

    What systems publish for others to act on: files, logs, events, confidential reports and required disclosures.

    5 sessions, show sample readings
    • The Log: What every software engineer should know about real-time data’s unifying abstraction Kreps, 2013
    • Aviation Safety Reporting System: program summary NASA Ames for the FAA; established April 1976

  4. IV Seeing hardness in incidents

    Investigations show where hardness held, where it was missing, and where it cracked.

    5 sessions, show sample readings
    • Report of the President’s Commission on the Accident at Three Mile Island October 1979, "Attitudes and Practices"
    • Final Report on the accident on 1st June 2009 to the Airbus A330-203, flight AF 447 BEA, 2012
    • Postmortem Culture: Learning from Failure Google SRE book, ch. 15

  5. V Designing the hard core

    Guardrails built into the architecture, from end-to-end design to staged rollouts, checklists and signed mandates for agents.

    6 sessions, show sample readings
    • Summary of the Amazon S3 Service Disruption in the Northern Virginia (US-EAST-1) Region Amazon Web Services, 2017
    • External Technical Root Cause Analysis: Channel File 291 CrowdStrike, 2024
    • Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) Google Cloud, 16 Sep 2025

  6. VI Keeping the core alive, and the contrast

    How industries keep amending their protocols, read against re-engineering’s clean slate.

    3 sessions, show sample readings
    • Reengineering Work: Don’t Automate, Obliterate Hammer, 1990
    • 14 CFR Part 5, Safety Management Systems FAA, 2015, expanded 2024

What happens in a session

  1. Silent reading of the core text or an excerpt.
  2. Round-robin on the reading: one reaction from each person.
  3. Show-and-tell: a tool someone is using, a member's case study progress, or a guest speaker.

Alongside the session, members post the protocols they've been watching in #protocols-for-business.

Suggest a reading

Your AI assistant knows what you’ve read, worked on and talked through. Copy the prompt into it, check what it suggests, and it will add each reading to the map on GitHub. Each contribution adds one participant’s experience to a shared picture of the field.

Read the prompt first 
I’m part of the Protocols for Business SIG at the Protocol Institute. We’re building a shared map of readings on how businesses coordinate through protocols between people, software and machines. The year’s syllabus has six themes: what agents are in practice; natural coordination; emissions, meaning what systems publish for others to act on; seeing hardness in incidents; designing the hard core; and keeping the core alive, set against business process re-engineering.

The readings already on the map are listed here: https://npc.here.now/protocolvision/sessions/map/readings.json

Use what you know about me: your memory of me, our past conversations, my projects and work, and anything I’ve read, saved or written about. From that context, suggest up to five readings the map is missing, especially ones that have shaped how I work. Prefer primary sources: incident and accident reports, specifications, regulations, original research papers, and first-hand accounts from people who ran systems at scale. Only include sources you’re confident exist, and leave out anything private about me, my employer or other people.

For each reading, give the title, author and year, a working link, the theme it fits, one sentence on what it adds to the map, and one sentence on why it matters from my own experience. Show me the list and wait for me to confirm or edit it.

Then add the confirmed readings to the map on GitHub, one issue per reading, in the repository protocolvision/sig-p4b:
- If you can create GitHub issues for me, open them directly. Use the title “Reading: ” followed by the reading’s title, add the label reading-suggestion, and put the fields above in the body.
- If you can’t, give me one link per reading that opens a filled-in form, with each value URL-encoded:
https://github.com/protocolvision/sig-p4b/issues/new?template=reading-suggestion.yml&title=Reading:%20TITLE&reading_title=TITLE&url=LINK&author=AUTHOR%20AND%20YEAR&adds=WHAT%20IT%20ADDS&experience=WHY%20IT%20MATTERS

Your assistant opens a GitHub issue for each reading, or gives you a link to a filled-in form. You’ll need a free GitHub account. You can also use the form yourself, and talk it through in #protocols-for-business on Discord.
