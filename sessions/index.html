How a session runs

  1. Silent reading of the core text or an excerpt.
  2. Round-robin on the reading: one reaction from each person.
  3. Show-and-tell: a tool someone is using, a member's case study progress, or a guest speaker.

Alongside the session, members post the protocols they've been watching in #protocols-for-business.

Syllabus

The question for the year: how do organizations, teams, and people coordinate through what they emit, and what changes when agents read and write those signals too?

To suggest a reading, mention it in #protocols-for-business on Discord.

Nov 2026I. Framing: recent swarms

Recent swarms. What two frontier labs reported about agents coordinating, and failing to, in 2026. Alongside: Securing Stigmergic Systems (Patrick Nast, Protocolized, 2026)

  • 2 November 2026 The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead OpenAI, 2026
    with Security incident disclosure — July 2026 Hugging Face, 2026
    show-and-tell Kickoff: how the year runs, and choosing your case     The models first found ways to communicate by writing files into the Artifactory package manager. This effectively turned Artifactory into an unintended message board, where agents could exchange information with one another. To understand what a swarm of tens of thousands of automated actions did, we ran LLM-driven analysis agents over the full attacker action log, comprised of more than 17,000 recorded events.
  • 16 November 2026 Patterns and problems in multiagent systems Anthropic, 2026
    show-and-tell Guest: Formal Protocol Theory SIG (Stigmergic AI Safety)     We consistently saw a multiagent turf war. All of the models we tested quickly assumed that others were purposefully impeding their work, and began to sabotage others while protecting their own contributions.

What we now see
Agent swarms are already here. In one incident, hundreds of instances coordinated through message boards they improvised. In another study, agents that couldn't see each other assumed hostility and sabotaged each other. The question is not whether swarms form, but what they coordinate through.

Nov–Dec 2026II. Swarms are nature

Broadcast, trace, threshold. How colonies and cells coordinate with no one in charge. Alongside: Pond brains and GPT-4 (Gordon Brander, Squishy Computer, 2023)

  • 30 November 2026 The Ecology of Collective Behavior Gordon, 2014
    show-and-tell Case pitches: one paragraph each     In most ant species studied so far it seems that the allocation of tasks, or functions, to ants, uses a distributed system; changes in conditions shift the rate of local interaction that regulates ant activity
  • 14 December 2026 Quorum Sensing in Bacteria Miller & Bassler, 2001
    show-and-tell Guest: Distributed Robotics Group (Decentralized Robot Markets)     Quorum sensing bacteria produce and release chemical signal molecules called autoinducers that increase in concentration as a function of cell density. The detection of a minimal threshold stimulatory concentration of an autoinducer leads to an alteration in gene expression.

What we now see
Swarms are closer to ant colonies and bacteria than to civilizations. They coordinate through signals emitted into a shared environment, regulated by encounter rates, decay, and thresholds, not by intent or culture.

    show-and-tell Guest: Psychohistory SIG (Prophecy Protocols)     The log is the natural data structure for handling data flow between systems. The recipe is very simple: Take all the organization's data and put it into a central log for real-time subscription.

Boards and notes. The intermediate traces: shared boards and crowd notes. Alongside: Unprotocolized Knowledge (Kara Kittel & Toby Shorin, Summer of Protocols, 2024)

  • 22 March 2027 Birdwatch: Crowd Wisdom and Bridging Algorithms can Inform Understanding and Reduce the Spread of Misinformation Wojcik et al., 2022
    show-and-tell Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling     It could help set better norms for crowd-based discussions of social media posts by rewarding content that is informative and appreciated by a wide range of people.

Who's listening. Publish/subscribe, from the first information bus to agents. Alongside: Agents are actors (Gordon Brander, Squishy Computer, 2026)

  • 5 April 2027 The Information Bus: An Architecture for Extensible Distributed Systems Oki, Pfluegl, Siegel & Skeen, 1993
    show-and-tell Case study progress     To disseminate data objects, data producers generate them, label them with an appropriate subject, and publish them on the Information Bus. To receive such objects, data consumers subscribe to the same subject.
  • 19 April 2027 Model Context Protocol Specification modelcontextprotocol.io, 2024–
    show-and-tell Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling     subscriptions/listen opens a long-lived notification stream from the server to the client. Unlike one-off requests, the stream stays open and delivers notifications until the client cancels it.

What we now see
Business information moved from files to flows, and then into an intermediate layer of logs, events, feeds, boards, and notes. That middle layer is where coordination now happens, and where agents read and write.

  • 28 June 2027 Designing Freedom Beer, 1973
    show-and-tell Show-and-tell: AI-native tooling     What is it that controls variety? The answer is dead simple: variety. Variety absorbs variety, and nothing else can.
  • 12 July 2027 Form 8-K: General Instructions U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission

What we now see
People, teams, and organizations are emitters too: prices, costly signals, heedful attention, alarms, and mandated disclosures. Each already has conventions for what gets broadcast, and who is obliged to listen.

Measure, then bootstrap. Measuring a swarm honestly, then improving how we improve. Alongside: Goodhart's Law and Why Measurement is Hard (David Manheim, Ribbonfarm, 2016)

  • 18 October 2027 Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change Campbell, 1976
    with Your AI Product Needs Evals Husain, 2024
    show-and-tell Case study progress     The more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures and the more apt it will be to distort and corrupt the social processes it is intended to monitor. I've found that unsuccessful products almost always share a common root cause: a failure to create robust evaluation systems.
  • 1 November 2027 Improving Our Ability to Improve Engelbart, 2003
    show-and-tell Thesis session: drafting the AI-native, post-platform organization     When you are operating at the C-level, collaboration can produce much larger returns than competition.

What we now see
Governing a swarm means tending its environment: what is emitted, who can read it, how fast it decays, and what triggers action. It also means measuring without letting the measure become the target. From there, the group writes its thesis.
