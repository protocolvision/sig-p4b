# AI Kitcraft, 21–22 September 2026

::: generated
Last week the SIG ran [AI Kitcraft](https://ai.protocolized.dev/kitcraft/), a two-day hands-on workshop at the 2026 Protocol Symposium, facilitated by Rafa and Sachin. Twelve participants each worked with their own AI assistant inside a shared [public repository](https://github.com/protocolvision/workshop-kitkraft). Their assistants read from the repo, wrote to it, and kept a log of what they did. The workshop followed one spine, **Kit → Factory → Bridge**:

- **Kit.** Take stock of the tools and habits you already work with. Almost everyone wrote an inventory, which gives us a small, honest snapshot of how people in our network actually use AI day to day.
- **Factory.** Turn one repeatable piece of your work into something your assistant can run: a written skill with example inputs and outputs. Four participants packaged a full factory, and two more produced specs and a working demo.
- **Bridge.** Connect your factory to someone else's. This was the open question the workshop ended on, and it stayed open: one bridge was written. Getting two people's AI setups to work together turned out to be much harder than building either one.

That last point is the useful result. Individual tooling is getting easy, and coordination between setups is where the work is. That is the same question our [research](https://npc.here.now/protocolvision/research/) asks about agents inside organizations. The repo stays up as a public record, and the bridge question carries into this year's sessions.
