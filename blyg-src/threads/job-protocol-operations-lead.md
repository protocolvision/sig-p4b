# A job description for the person who runs your protocols

::: generated
Companies putting AI agents to work keep running into the same wall. The rules that decide how fast they can move (who can approve a deal, how much can be spent, who can see what, what data can leave) live in people's heads. So every new agent, partner and product waits on a manual sign-off, and nobody's job is to change that.

Security, finance and legal decide what the rules say. Engineering builds whatever it is asked to build. Sales lives with the result. What no one owns is the whole life of a rule: which few rules matter most, whether a system enforces them, who may change them, and whether they still fit the business a quarter later. In [Business Protocol Management](https://protocolsforbusiness.com/research/bpm/) terms, that is the hard core, and a hard core without an owner drifts back into people's heads.

Below is a job description for that owner, a Protocol Operations Lead. A few choices in it are deliberate:

- **It reports to the COO**, not to security or engineering. The rules exist to let the business move, not only to limit risk. Under security, the role tends to only tighten. Under engineering, it tends to become a platform project.
- **It measures speed and non-events together**: deal and request cycle time, decisions made without manual approval, time to onboard a customer, partner or agent, and incidents caught or prevented.
- **It treats rules as something others build on.** One duty is to publish approval, pricing and data-request interfaces that teams, partners and agents can use without a new integration project. A good rule stops the wrong things and makes the right things easy to rely on.
- **It removes rules as well as adding them.** Each quarter, tighten what keeps failing and drop what slows work without reducing risk.
- **The first 90 days end in one enforced rule**, with cycle time measured before and after. An inventory alone doesn't count.

The title and the word "protocols" are ours. If your company already calls this revenue operations, internal controls or business systems, keep your word and keep the duties.

## Protocol Operations Lead

[Company] · [Location] · Reports to the Chief Operations Officer

### About the role

[Company] is putting AI agents to work across [sales, customer operations and finance]. How fast we grow depends on a few rules: who can approve a deal, how much can be spent, who can access what, and what data can go to a customer or partner. When those rules live in people's heads, every new agent, partner and product waits on a manual sign-off. When our systems enforce them, teams and agents can act without asking. We call these rules our protocols.

You will own how those rules are built into our systems, monitored and changed. Security, finance and legal decide what the rules say. Engineering builds them. Sales and go-to-market teams rely on them to quote, close and onboard customers. You make sure each rule has an owner, runs in a system, and keeps pace with the business.

### What you'll do

- Map how work gets approved, paid, accessed and released across [functions], and show which rules a system enforces today and which depend on a person remembering.
- Agree with the COO which few rules come first, weighing the revenue each one holds up against the risk it carries. Typical candidates are signing authority, discount and spending limits, payment approval, access rights, change control, outbound data, output checks and audit logging.
- Work with sales and go-to-market teams to remove manual sign-offs from quoting, discounting, contracting and customer onboarding, so deals close inside pre-approved limits.
- Work with security to review each new agent before launch: what it can see, do and spend, who owns it, and how to stop it.
- Work with engineering to build the rules into [CRM, ERP, payments, identity and agent platforms], so they apply to every person and agent.
- Publish approval, pricing and data-request interfaces that teams, partners and agents can build on without a new integration project.
- Keep a shared log of agent and system actions, and run blameless reviews of incidents and near-misses that end in a system change.
- Review the rules with their owners every [quarter]. Tighten what keeps failing, and remove what slows work without reducing risk.
- Report to the COO on deal and request cycle time, decisions made without manual approval, time to onboard a new customer, partner or agent, and incidents caught or prevented.

### What you'll bring

- [5–8] years in operations, revenue operations, internal controls, business systems, reliability or platform engineering.
- A record of taking a written policy and getting it enforced in a system: permissions, approval workflows, pricing or spending limits, or release gates.
- Hands-on experience running AI agents or workflow automation in production, using [n8n, Workato, an agent platform or model APIs].
- Working knowledge of [CRM and ERP], identity and access management, and API integrations. You can read logs and agent traces.
- Experience leading incident reviews that changed how a system works.
- Experience getting security, engineering and sales or go-to-market teams to agree on a rule and its trade-offs.

### Nice to have

- SQL or Python.
- Background in audit, SOX or ISO 27001 controls, site reliability or DevOps.
- Experience in deal desk, revenue operations or partner integrations.
- Experience in [industry].

### Your first 90 days

- **Day 30:** an inventory of the approvals, permissions and data flows behind [one revenue workflow, such as quote-to-contract], showing which ones a system enforces.
- **Day 60:** the three rules that most slow or expose that workflow, each with a named owner and a written change process.
- **Day 90:** one of those rules enforced in a system, with cycle time measured before and after.
